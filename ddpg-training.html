Training DDPG: Target Networks, Replay and Noise

Three fixes that make Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) learn to control a robot arm.

Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG), run without the slow copies of its networks, the memory of past steps and the random nudges its own page already used, learns toward numbers that shift with every update, learns from back-to-back steps that look alike, and never tries anything new. Left alone, these faults can make the arm's learning blow up or stall. This page teaches the three fixes that make it train.

The agent is the learner that picks the moves. (RL Basics) A state is the agent's situation right now. A move, or action, is one choice the agent can make in a state. A reward is the number the world hands back after each move, good or bad. (RL Basics)

On a robot, sensing, moving and scoring repeat many times every second.

The running example is the Reacher task: a robot arm with two joints whose actions are four torques. A torque is a turning force a motor applies at a joint. On the Reacher each torque is a number from -1 to 1. Each joint can turn about two axes, so each joint gets two torques. Every step the arm senses its state, numbers describing the position, rotation and speed of its parts, and earns a small reward whenever its hand is inside a moving goal. My repo trains 20 such arms side by side. (Continuous-Action RL)

The return is all the reward still to come, with later rewards counted a little less. (RL Basics) A move's value \(Q(s,a)\), also called its score, is the return the agent expects if it makes move \(a\) in state \(s\) and plays on from there. (RL Basics)

DDPG learns two networks for the arm.

The deterministic actor \(\mu_\theta\) is a network with weights \(\theta\) that outputs one action every time, with no random draw. The critic \(Q_\phi\) is a network with weights \(\phi\) that scores a state and an action together; its score stands for the return the agent expects. (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG): Actor and Critic)

The actor proposes the arm's torques, and the critic tells it which way to change them to score higher.

1 The fixes that keep DDPG training stable

Slow target copies, a replay buffer and exploration noise each fix one of these.

1.1 Copies that change slowly

The critic learns by being pulled toward a target, the step's reward plus its own score for what comes next, counted a little less. (Continuous-Action RL)

That target moves whenever the critic moves.

The Deep Q-Network (DQN) method scores moves with one network, its Q-network. DQN had the same problem of a target that moves, and fixed it with a second network.

On the arm, a target that moves would let the critic's scores chase their own changes.

DQN and DDPG are both value methods: they learn by scoring moves. The target network is a slow copy of the Q-network, with weights \(\phi'\), that computes the targets. (Deep Q-Networks (DQN)) Slow means the copy, written with a prime on its weights, is updated only a little at a time. In this family the critic plays the Q-network's part. (Training DDPG)

This holds the target still while the critic learns.

In DDPG both the actor and the critic have a slow copy, marked by a prime on the weights, and the targets are built from those copies. (Training DDPG) The discount \(\gamma\) sets how much a reward one step later still counts. (RL Basics) The DDPG critic's target is the step's reward plus the discounted score the slow critic gives to the slow actor's next torques. (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG): Actor and Critic)

On the arm, each slow copy must still follow the network it copies, or the critic would learn from stale scores.

The live networks are the actor and critic being trained, with weights \(\theta\) and \(\phi\). (Training DDPG) The soft update blends a small share \(\tau\) of the live weights into each slow copy after every update, instead of overwriting it. (Deep Q-Networks (DQN))

So a slow copy trails its live network by many updates.

Equation 1. Slow copies follow the live networks. After each update on a Reacher batch, the slow critic and slow actor drift a small way toward the live ones. The target is built from copies that follow the live networks slowly, so the critic is not chasing its own moving answer.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\phi'} \leftarrow \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\tau}\,\htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{\phi} + (1-\htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\tau})\,\htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\phi'}\)
The slow critic's weights \(\phi'\) are replaced by a blend of a small share \(\tau\) of the live critic's weights \(\phi\) and the rest of the slow copy's own weights. With \(\tau\) small, each slow weight moves only a hair per update; the rest of the slow copy stays as it was; blending in all of the live weights at once would be DQN's hard copy. The arrow means the left side is replaced by the right side.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\theta'} \leftarrow \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\tau}\,\htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{\theta} + (1-\htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\tau})\,\htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\theta'}\)
The slow actor's weights \(\theta'\) are replaced by the same blend of the live actor's weights \(\theta\) and their own. These copies are the ones inside the DDPG critic's target. It builds on DQN's hard copy (Deep Q-Networks (DQN)) and soft update (Deep Q-Networks (DQN)), and the DDPG page's second equation (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG): Actor and Critic).

A hard copy overwrites the slow copy with the live weights every so often. (Training DDPG)

Raising the share makes the slow copies follow the live networks faster, and the target wobbles more; lowering it holds the target still but slows learning.

The code below is the soft update: it loops over the weights and blends a small share of the live weights into each slow copy.

TAU = SOFT_UPDATE_RATE
@torch.no_grad()
def soft_update(tgt, live):
    dst = list(tgt.parameters())
    src = list(live.parameters())
    for t, p in zip(dst, src):
        t.lerp_(p, TAU)
soft_update(critic_target, critic)
soft_update(actor_target, actor)
  1. 1Pairs each slow weight with its live weight and loops over the pairs.
  2. 2Blends a small share of each live weight into its slow copy, once for the critic pair and once for the actor pair.

1.2 A replay buffer of past steps

Replay keeps a memory of past steps, and training draws random batches from it. (Deep Q-Networks (DQN))

Back-to-back steps look alike, so a network trained on them fits the last few and forgets the rest. Sampling at random from a large memory mixes old and new.

The code below stores steps in a memory and draws a random batch from it.

import random
from collections import deque

BUFFER_SIZE, BATCH = BUFFER_CAPACITY, BATCH_SIZE
buf = deque(maxlen=BUFFER_SIZE)
step = (s, a, r, s_next, done)
buf.append(step)
batch = random.sample(buf, BATCH)
  1. 1Creates the memory and stores one step in it.
  2. 2Draws a random batch from the memory.

Many arms fill it much faster than one.

1.3 Random nudges with memory

Exploration noise is a small random nudge added to the actor's torques while it learns, so the arm tries moves it is not sure about. (Training DDPG) Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) noise is a nudge with memory: each step it keeps most of the last nudge, is pulled back toward zero, and gets a fresh random kick. (Training DDPG)

It is named after the two physicists who described it (Uhlenbeck and Ornstein, 1930).

Because each nudge keeps most of the last, the arm sweeps smoothly instead of twitching.

Equation 2. A nudge with memory. Each of the arm's four torques gets its own nudge that drifts smoothly over several steps, so the arm sweeps instead of twitching. Noise with memory: each step it keeps most of the last nudge, is pulled back toward zero, and gets a fresh kick.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{x} \leftarrow \htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{x} - \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\kappa}\,\htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{x} + \htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{\sigma_{\mathrm{OU}}}\,\htmlStyle{color:#8e44ad}{\xi}\)
\(x\) is the current nudge, one per torque. \(\kappa\) is the pull back to zero: a bigger pull brings each nudge back faster. \(\sigma_{\mathrm{OU}}\) is the size of each kick. \(\xi\) is a fresh standard normal draw. (Training DDPG) The DDPG paper and the repo use the values in the settings table for the Reacher arms.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{a} = \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\mu_\theta}(\htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{s}) + \htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{x}\)
The torques sent to the arm start as the actor's torques plus this nudge. It builds on exploration (RL Basics) and the DDPG page's first equation, line 2 (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG): Actor and Critic).
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{a} \leftarrow \operatorname{clip}(\htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{a},\ -1,\ 1)\)
The torques are then clipped into -1 to 1, so no torque asks for more than the arm can give. The DDPG paper simply adds the nudge; the repo also clips each torque into -1 to 1.

The DDPG paper calls the pull \(\theta\); we write \(\kappa\) so \(\theta\) stays the actor's weights.

Raising the pull brings each nudge back toward zero faster, so the arm wanders less far; lowering it lets each nudge drift longer. Raising the size of each kick makes the nudges bigger, so the arm tries wider moves; lowering it makes the nudges smaller and the arm stays closer to what it already does.

In the repo, the nudge is added inside the step that picks the torques, its act step, so the update code on this page calls the actor directly and defines no noise helper, a small routine that only makes the nudge.

The slow copies move a small step toward the live networks.

An episode is one run from the start until it ends. (RL Basics) The code marks where each arm's episode ended, so no future score is added past it.

The update below runs one training update on a sampled batch, in four parts.

First, the target for each step comes from the slow copies.

import torch.nn.functional as F
import torch.nn.utils as U

GAMMA, CLIP = DISCOUNT, CLIP_LIMIT

@torch.no_grad()
def td_target(r, s_next, done):
    a2 = actor_target(s_next)
    q2 = critic_target(s_next, a2)
    keep = 1 - done
    return r + GAMMA * q2 * keep
  1. 1Builds each step's target from the slow copies; done marks the end of an episode, so the target adds no future value there (the DDPG critic's target, on the DDPG page).

Next, the critic moves toward those targets.

def critic_step(batch):
    s, a, r, s_next, done = batch
    y = td_target(r, s_next, done)
    q = critic(s, a)
    critic_loss = F.mse_loss(q, y)
    critic_opt.zero_grad()
    critic_loss.backward()
    U.clip_grad_norm_(critic.parameters(), CLIP)
    critic_opt.step()
  1. 1Moves the critic toward those targets (the DDPG critic's loss, on the DDPG page).

Then the actor moves so its torques shift the way the critic's score rises.

def actor_step(s):
    q = critic(s, actor(s))
    actor_loss = -q.mean()
    actor_opt.zero_grad()
    actor_loss.backward()
    actor_opt.step()
  1. 1Moves the actor so its torques shift the way the critic's score rises (the DDPG actor update, on the DDPG page).

Last, both slow copies blend a small step toward the live networks.

soft_update(actor_target, actor)
soft_update(critic_target, critic)
  1. 1Blends both slow copies a small step toward the live networks, with the soft_update from Code 1 (Equation 1 on this page).

Every reward here is a cost for being far from the goal and for moving hard, so the critic, which starts at zero, is too hopeful at first; its scores fall as it learns what the costs are, while the arm already reaches well.

The critic must chase a slowly moving copy of itself; chasing its live self, its scores run away.

5 When each stabilizer matters and how training fails

Table 2. When each stabilizer matters
StabilizerWhen it matters most
Slow copiesWhen the critic's target is built from its own fast-changing scores. Soft blends beat hard copies when updates are frequent and small, because the target never jumps; a hard copy is simpler when updates are rare.
ReplayWhen consecutive steps look alike, as with many arms moving smoothly. A smaller memory adapts faster when the task changes.
Exploration noiseEarly, when the actor has never seen the goal region. Turn it down as the arm improves and switch it off at evaluation.

DQN's target takes a max, the highest of the scores its Q-network gives the next moves.

Overestimation means the values climb above the return the agent really earns, because a max over noisy guesses picks lucky ones. (Double DQN)

In DDPG the actor plays the role of the max, because it learns to pick the torques the critic scores highest.

Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3), a later method, keeps two critics and trusts the lower score, which curbs this.

Most other failures come from one of the three fixes set too weak or too strong.

6 Methods that build on DDPG

Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3) keeps two critics, trusts the lower score, and delays the actor updates. It also found that noise drawn fresh each step works as well as the nudge with memory.

Soft Actor-Critic learns a range of torques, and is rewarded for keeping it wide.

Multi-agent DDPG trains several arms or players; each agent's critic sees all the agents.

Each of them still learns from a replay memory of past steps.
