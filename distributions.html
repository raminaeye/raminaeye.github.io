Bernoulli, Categorical and Uniform Distributions

A map of every probability shape the site uses, then the yes/no, one-of-K and flat shapes: the loss each gives and how a model draws from them.

1 Every shape the site uses

Every model output on the site is a chance over something, and the shape of that chance depends on what is predicted: a yes or no, one of \(K\) choices, a value in a range, or a real number. The minus log of the shape is the training loss (section 5).

shapewhat it describeswhere on the siteloss from its minus log
Bernoullione yes or no, with chance \(q\) of a 1logistic regression, the dropout keep maskbinary cross-entropy (BCE)
categoricalone of \(K\) choices, one chance eachthe next token in decoding, naive Bayescross-entropy
uniforma value anywhere in a range, equally likelyconverter rounding error (ADC), the point \(u\) behind every drawnot used as a loss
Gaussianthe bell, written \(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\), taught on The Gaussian Distributiondiffusion noise, GMM, PSDsquared error
2-D Gaussiana tilted cloud over several numbers (The Gaussian Distribution, section 7)GMM shapes, the Wiener filtersquared distance in the cloud’s own units
Student-ta bump with fat tails (The Gaussian Distribution, section 6)t-SNE mapsrobust regression losses

How often each runs: a Bernoulli once per label per example in logistic regression, or once per unit per training step in dropout; a categorical once per generated token and once per example in the loss; a uniform draw inside every sample. Each carries only its parameters: \(q\), the \(K\) chances, or the two ends of the range.

2 One yes or no is a Bernoulli

A logistic classifier gives the chance that a forum post comes from the hockey forum. A dropout keep mask is a 1 to keep a unit and a 0 to zero it, drawn fresh for every unit at every training step (Srivastava et al. 2014). Both are one yes or no with a chance \(q\) of a 1: a Bernoulli.

One formula gives the chance of both outcomes.
\(\displaystyle P(x) = \htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{q}^{x}\,(1-\htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{q})^{1-x}\)
Capital \(X\) is the outcome, a random variable, and lowercase \(x\) is the value it takes, a 1 or a 0. \(q\) is the chance of a 1. The exponents switch one factor off, so a 1 leaves \(q\) and a 0 leaves \(1-q\). For dropout, \(q\) counts keeps, not drops.
\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}[X] = \htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{q}\)
The average outcome is the chance of a 1: over many posts, the share labelled hockey.
\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Var}(X) = \htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{q}\,(1-\htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{q})\)
The spread is largest when a 1 and a 0 are equally likely and vanishes when the outcome is certain (Figure 2). A classifier sitting at unsure gives the noisiest labels.

3 Softmax makes a categorical

After “The cat sat on the”, a language model gives every word in its vocabulary a raw score, a logit \(z\). Softmax turns the scores into chances, a categorical over the \(K\) choices. The same step gives attention weights, multiclass logistic regression and the cross-entropy loss.

Softmax turns any scores into positive chances that add to one; only the gaps between the scores matter.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{P(k)} = \dfrac{\exp(\htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{z_k})}{\sum_{m=1}^{K} \exp(z_m)}\)
\(z_k\) is choice \(k\)’s logit, its raw score from the model, and \(K\) is the number of choices. \(\exp\) makes every score positive, and the bottom is the same sum over all \(K\) choices, so each chance is its share of that sum. Adding one constant to every logit scales the top and the bottom alike and changes nothing; that is why libraries subtract the largest logit before \(\exp\), so it never overflows.
\(\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{K} \htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{P(k)} = 1\)
The chances add to one because the bottom of the fraction is the sum of all the tops.
\(\displaystyle \sigma(z) = \dfrac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}\)
With two choices, one with logit \(z\) and the other held at zero, softmax gives the first this chance: the sigmoid \(\sigma\) of logistic regression. A Bernoulli is the two-choice categorical, with \(q = \sigma(z)\) in the Bernoulli formula of section 2.

Argmax picks the choice with the largest chance, which is greedy decoding; drawing instead, and reshaping the chances first with temperature, are on Sampling the Next Token. That page owns the reshaping; this one owns the shape.

4 A uniform is flat over a range

An analog-to-digital converter (ADC), the chip that turns a microphone voltage into numbers, rounds each value to the nearest step. The rounding error lands anywhere within half a step either way, all spots equally likely: a uniform. Every sampler on the site also starts from a uniform point (section 6).

The height is whatever makes the area one.
\(\displaystyle p(x) = \dfrac{1}{\htmlStyle{color:#ca6f1e}{b} - \htmlStyle{color:#ca6f1e}{a}}\)
\(a\) and \(b\) are the ends of the range; the height holds for every \(x\) between them and is zero outside. The height is one over the width, so a wider range is a lower block and the area stays one. Lowercase \(p\) is a density: its area over a stretch is a chance.
\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Var}(X) = \dfrac{(\htmlStyle{color:#ca6f1e}{b} - \htmlStyle{color:#ca6f1e}{a})^{2}}{12}\)
The variance is the average squared distance from the middle of the block. It grows with the width squared (Figure 4), and for a flat block that average comes out as one twelfth of the width squared. For one converter step the width is the step size, and this is the rounding-noise power that ADC uses (Bennett 1948).

5 Losses are minus log-likelihoods

The likelihood is the chance a model gave to what actually happened. Training minimizes its minus natural log, \(-\ln\), which is zero for a certain hit and grows without limit as that chance falls to zero.

shapewhat the model outputsloss
Bernoullia chance \(q\) of a 1binary cross-entropy
categoricalone chance per choice, from softmaxcross-entropy
Gaussian, spread fixeda prediction \(\hat{y}\), the bell’s centrehalf the squared error
Each minus log is a training loss: the less chance the model gave the truth, the larger it is.
\(\displaystyle \mathrm{BCE} = -\ln P(y)\)
Binary cross-entropy (BCE) is the minus log of the Bernoulli chance of the true label \(y\), 1 for hockey and 0 otherwise.
\(\displaystyle = -\ln \htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{q} \quad \text{if } y = 1\)
Written out with \(q\), the model’s chance of hockey: a hockey label costs minus the log of \(q\).
\(\displaystyle = -\ln(1-\htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{q}) \quad \text{if } y = 0\)
Any other label costs minus the log of the chance left over. Libraries fold both cases into one line with \(y\) switching between them.
\(\displaystyle \mathrm{CE} = -\ln \htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{P(k)}\)
For a categorical, \(k\) is the true choice, such as the word that actually came next, and the minus log of its chance is the cross-entropy loss, averaged over a batch.
\(\displaystyle \mathcal{L} = \dfrac{(y-\hat{y})^{2}}{2} + \text{const}\)
For a real-valued target, the model’s prediction \(\hat{y}\) is the centre of a bell whose spread is held fixed at one, taught on The Gaussian Distribution, and \(\mathcal{L}\) is the minus log of that bell’s height at the target, \(-\ln \mathcal{N}(y \mid \hat{y}, 1)\). It is half the squared error plus a constant, half of \(\ln 2\pi\), that never changes the best prediction, so minimizing it is regression’s squared error. With a learned spread it becomes the loss of The Gaussian Distribution, section 5.

Maximum Likelihood and EM builds on these losses. KL Divergence is the cross-entropy minus the entropy.

6 Drawing a sample

Builds on softmax (section 3) and the uniform (section 4)

Sampled tokens in decoding, dropout masks, k-means++ starts, subword sampling splits and the actions of policy gradients all come from one mechanism: a uniform point dropped on a strip of chances.

6.1 One draw

Lay the chances end to end on a strip of length one, drop a uniform point on it, and take the choice whose segment it lands in.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{F_j} = \sum_{m=1}^{j} \htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{P(m)}\)
The running total \(F_j\) adds the chances of the first \(j\) choices. It climbs from zero to one along the strip and marks where segment \(j\) ends.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#7d3c98}{u} \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)\)
The tilde reads “is drawn from”: \(u\) is a point from the flat block of section 4, anywhere on the strip with equal chance.
\(\displaystyle k^{*} = \min\{\, j : \htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{F_j} \ge \htmlStyle{color:#7d3c98}{u} \,\}\)
\(k^{*}\) is the drawn choice, the first \(j\) whose running total reaches \(u\). Each segment is exactly as long as its chance, so each choice is drawn exactly that often.

The first segment whose end passes \(u\) is the draw.
\(\displaystyle P(\text{keep}) = \htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{q}\)
A strip with two segments is one Bernoulli draw: the keep mask of dropout.

6.2 Many draws match the chances

One draw can land anywhere, but because each segment is exactly as long as its chance, the share of draws that land in it settles onto that chance as draws pile up.

6.3 In PyTorch

PyTorch’s distribution classes build each shape from its parameters, score an outcome in logs, and draw.

import torch
from torch.distributions import Bernoulli, Categorical, Uniform

vocab = ["mat", "floor", "sofa", "rug", "bed"]   # next words after "The cat sat on the"
logits = torch.randn(len(vocab))                 # stand-in for the model's scores
next_word = Categorical(logits=logits)           # applies softmax itself
draw = next_word.sample()
loss = -next_word.log_prob(torch.tensor(vocab.index("mat")))

drop_rate = 0.1                                  # a setting: the chance to drop a unit
hidden = torch.randn_like(logits)                # stand-in for a layer's outputs
keep = Bernoulli(probs=torch.full_like(hidden, 1 - drop_rate)).sample()
u = Uniform(0.0, 1.0).sample()
  1. 1logits are the softmax scores \(z\) of section 3, and sample() makes one draw as in Figure 6.
  2. 2The minus log_prob of the true word is the cross-entropy of section 5. log_prob works in logs, so a tiny chance never rounds to zero first.
  3. 3One keep mask, a Bernoulli per unit (section 2) whose \(q\) counts keeps, while torch.nn.Dropout(p) takes the drop chance; u is the uniform point \(u\) of section 6.1.

Drawing from a bell is a different mechanism, a standard draw stretched and slid, on The Gaussian Distribution, section 4.

Compute in log space, keep logits as the model’s outputs, and check in tests that the chances sum to one.

8 Where the bell comes in

Real-valued noise, regression losses and speech filters all use one shape. What makes the bell so common, and how does it handle several numbers that move together? That is The Gaussian Distribution.
