1 One block, three stacks

The same block builds three kinds of model, differing in the self-attention mask and whether a second stream comes in.

Each stack runs its blocks on every forward pass; what changes is what each position may see. An encoder-only stack reads and understands: every position sees the whole sentence. An encoder-decoder stack translates: the decoder writes while reading the source. A decoder-only stack writes: each position sees only what came before, and the stack predicts what comes next. The mask and the second stream are the only differences; the block itself never changes.

  • An encoder stack lets every token see every other token, past and future. BERT (Devlin et al. 2019) is encoder-only.
  • A decoder stack uses the causal mask, so each token sees only itself and earlier tokens. GPT (Radford et al., 2018), OpenAI’s family of text generators, is decoder-only.
  • The 2017 translator uses both. Its decoder blocks add a cross-attention sublayer, with queries from the decoder and keys and values from the encoder's output; Cross-Attention covers it.

2 Encoder-only: read everything, predict the hidden words

An encoder-only stack reads the whole sentence at once. Every token sees every other token, past and future, so the model is built for understanding text, not writing it. It never predicts a next word, because there is no next word in its job: it reads a sentence that is already complete and works out what it means.

BERT (Devlin et al., 2019) trains such a stack with masked-language modeling. Some words in each sentence are selected for prediction, 15 percent of them, and usually hidden behind a mask token: the paper replaces a chosen word with [MASK] 80 percent of the time, a random word 10 percent, and leaves it unchanged 10 percent. The model predicts the original word from its context, so it must use both sides: the words before and the words after. A second task asks whether one sentence follows another.

Hiding every chosen word behind [MASK] would teach the model a token it never meets in later use, so the paper varies the replacement instead.

The masked-language-modeling loss, cross entropy at the masked positions only.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-loss)}{L} = -\frac{1}{\htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{M}}\sum_{m=1}^{\htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{M}} \log P(x_{m} | x_{\setminus m})\)
The loss is low when the model gives the true hidden word a high probability at every masked position, given the rest of the sentence. \(M\) is how many positions are masked; \(x_{m}\) is the true word at masked position \(m\), and \(x_{\setminus m}\) is the sentence with that word left out. The sum runs over the masked positions only, divided by \(M\), so one sentence grades every hidden word at once. It extends the categorical cross-entropy card on Cross-Entropy.

France hides behind [MASK]; the model reads the words on both sides to fill it in.

The snippet gives toy scores whose top word is France, and grades the true word id behind [MASK].

That training suits understanding tasks. With one extra output layer on top of the stack, the same model answers questions, judges whether one sentence follows from another, and classifies text. Fine-tuning, training the pretrained stack further on the task, adapts it with that one added layer. BERT-base stacks 12 blocks and BERT-large 24. Question answering and language inference are the paper's examples.

BERT learned from BooksCorpus, a collection of books, and English Wikipedia. The same masking idea, applied to image patches instead of words, is taught on Masked Image Modeling.

3 Encoder-decoder: read one language, write another

The 2017 translator is an encoder-decoder: the encoder reads the source sentence, and the decoder writes the translation while reading the encoder through cross-attention.

The decoder works in three steps at each position: causal-masked attention over the words written so far, then cross-attention over the encoder's keys and values, then a Linear layer, which turns each position's vector into one score per word in the vocabulary, and Softmax, which turns the scores into probabilities. The base model beat the best convolutional ensemble on English to German but not on English to French, and the larger model beat it on both. The task is WMT 2014 English to German and English to French.

  • 1Input Embedding turns tokens into vectors and the plus circle adds the position signal; data flows upward.
  • 2The encoder reads the input: \(N\) blocks (Nx), each sublayer wrapped in the Post-LN Add & Norm.
  • 3The decoder writes the output: causal-masked attention, then attention over the encoder's keys and values.
  • 4Linear and Softmax turn the decoder's last vector into a probability for every vocabulary word.

What changes when one stream reads another, such as a translation reading its source? Cross-Attention.

4 Decoder-only: predict the next token at every position

Most models in use today, GPT among them, are decoder-only: one sequence, no encoder. The model predicts the next token, appends it and predicts again (autoregressive generation).

Training minimizes the cross entropy averaged over positions:

Each position is graded on the next word, so the targets shift by one.

The decoder-only training loss, cross entropy at every position, averaged.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-loss)}{L} = -\frac{1}{\htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{\htmlStyle{color:#1f618d}{T}} \log P(x_{t} | x_{<t})\)
The loss \(L\) is low when the model gives the real next token a high probability at every position. \(P\)(\(x_{t}\) | \(x_{<t}\)) is the probability the model gives the true token at position \(t\), seeing only the tokens before it. The \(-\log\) turns a probability of 1 into 0 loss and a probability near 0 into a large one, so confident misses cost the most; the sum over the \(T\) positions, where \(T\) is the sequence length, divided by \(T\), averages them, so one sequence grades \(T\) predictions at once.

MiniGPT wraps the Block class from The Transformer Block with a token table, a position table and a final LayerNorm, and reuses the token table as the output layer, giving logits, one raw score per vocabulary word.

import torch
import torch.nn as nn

MAX_LEN, N_HEADS = MAX_SEQ_LEN, HEAD_COUNT

class MiniGPT(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, V, d, N):  # vocab, width, blocks
        super().__init__()
        self.tok = nn.Embedding(V, d)
        self.pos = nn.Embedding(MAX_LEN, d)
        blocks = [Block(d, N_HEADS, 4*d) for _ in range(N)]
        self.body = nn.ModuleList(blocks)
        self.ln = nn.LayerNorm(d)  # final LayerNorm

    def forward(self, idx):  # idx (B, T) token ids
        T = idx.size(1)
        x = self.tok(idx) + self.pos.weight[:T]
        mask = torch.ones(T, T, dtype=torch.bool).triu(1)
        for block in self.body:
            x = block(x, mask)
        return self.ln(x) @ self.tok.weight.T  # (B, T, V)
  1. 1Token and position tables, the stack of blocks and a final LayerNorm, the decoder-only stack of Figure 1.
  2. 2Token vectors plus position vectors, the input of every row in Figure 3.
  3. 3The causal mask and the blocks in order, so each row of Figure 3 sees only earlier words.
  4. 4Final LayerNorm, the Pre-LN card in The Transformer Block, line 3, then scores over the vocabulary.

Weight tying makes the output layer reuse the embedding table (both are vocabulary size by model width), saving a large block of weights.

Training MiniGPT on random toy batches, grading each position against the next token.

import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

VOCAB, d, N, LR = VOCAB_SIZE, MODEL_WIDTH, N_BLOCKS, LEARNING_RATE
model = MiniGPT(VOCAB, d, N)
opt = torch.optim.AdamW(model.parameters(), lr=LR)
batches = [torch.randint(VOCAB, (BATCH_SIZE, SEQ_LEN)) for _ in range(N_BATCHES)]

for ids in batches:  # ids (B, T)
    logits = model(ids)
    pred = logits[:, :-1].flatten(0, 1)
    target = ids[:, 1:].flatten()  # one step later
    loss = F.cross_entropy(pred, target, reduction="mean")
    opt.zero_grad()
    loss.backward()
    opt.step()
  1. 1Model, AdamW optimizer and random batches of token ids, the \(x_{t}\) of the loss card in Section 4, line 1.
  2. 2Shifts by one so each position predicts the next token, the loss card in Section 4, line 1.
  3. 3One optimizer step lowers \(L\), the loss card in Section 4, line 1.

At generation a setting called temperature divides the last position's logits before the softmax. It sharpens or flattens the probabilities without changing their order. Sampling the Next Token explains it, with the equation.

In generation, a prompt goes in and comes back with n_new tokens added.

import torch

@torch.no_grad()
def generate(model, ids, n_new, tau=1.0, max_len=MAX_LEN):
    for _ in range(n_new):
        z = model(ids[:, -max_len:])[:, -1]  # (B, V)
        p = (z / tau).softmax(dim=-1)
        nxt = p.multinomial(1)  # one token per row
        ids = torch.cat([ids, nxt], dim=1)
    return ids
  1. 1Logits for the last position only, the \(z_{i}\) of the temperature card.
  2. 2Temperature \(\tau\) divides them before the softmax, the temperature card.
  3. 3Samples one token by the probabilities \(p_{i}\) of the temperature card, and appends it.

multinomial draws a token by those probabilities; Sampling the Next Token shows top-k, top-p and min-p, which sample from only the likeliest words. Each step recomputes every earlier token's keys and values; The KV Cache shows how a cache removes that.

5 Failure modes and tips

  • Causal mask missing in a decoder. Cause: each position sees the answer. Symptom: training loss falls to near zero but generation is garbage. Fix: a decoder-only model needs a causal mask passed in.
  • Decoder without cross-attention in translation. Cause: the decoder never reads the source. Symptom: fluent translations unrelated to the input. Fix: add the cross-attention sublayer.
  • Temperature at the extremes. Cause: \(\tau\) near 0 always picks the top word, very large \(\tau\) picks at random. Symptom: repetitive or incoherent output. Fix: keep \(\tau\) near 1 and use top-k or top-p.
  • Masking nothing, or everything, in MLM. Cause: the task is trivial or impossible. Symptom: the encoder learns nothing useful. Fix: mask a small share of the words, the paper's 15 percent.

Two things worth keeping: weight tying saves a large block of weights, and each generation step recomputes every earlier token's keys and values, which The KV Cache removes.
