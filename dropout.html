1 What dropout does and the problem it solves

A large network trained on a few hundred wristband recordings can memorize them. One way it does so is co-adaptation: a few hidden units learn to fix each other’s mistakes on exactly these recordings, and the next layer leans on that small team. The team breaks on a new wearer, so the train and validation losses drift apart (overfitting).

Dropout (Srivastava et al. 2014) makes every hidden unit unreliable during training. On each forward pass, one run of an input through the network, each unit is switched off at random with chance \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{p}}\). No unit can count on a partner being present, so each has to be useful on its own. Each pass trains a different thinned network; at test time the full network runs with every unit on.

It runs on every training forward pass, with a fresh random mask for every example and every layer. It keeps no state between passes, and it costs one random draw and one multiply per activation. In evaluation mode it does nothing.

The drop chance \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{p}}\) is the one knob. Raise it and dropout regularizes harder; too high and the layer cannot carry enough signal, so the model underfits. Lower it toward zero and dropout fades away. PyTorch’s default is 0.5.

2 The mask and the rescale

Take one hidden layer of the gesture classifier on a single pass. Some of its units are silenced; the rest pass their activations on, a little louder. The card writes that in three lines: draw the mask, apply it with a rescale, and check what the layer outputs on average.

A coin per unit, then silence the dropped units and scale up the kept ones.
\(\displaystyle {\htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{m_i}} \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}\big(1 - \htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{p}}\big)}\)
A weighted coin for unit \(i\) lands on keep with chance one minus \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{p}}\). The mask \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{m_i}}\) is one for a kept unit and zero for a dropped one, and \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{p}}\) is the drop chance, the meaning PyTorch uses.
\(\displaystyle {\htmlClass{eqt sym-layerout}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-layerout)}{\tilde{a}_i}} = \frac{\htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{m_i}}\, \htmlClass{eqt sym-layerout}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-layerout)}{a_i}}}{1 - \htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{p}}}}\)
Builds on line 1. \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-layerout}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-layerout)}{a_i}}\) is the unit’s activation before dropout. Multiplying by the mask zeroes the dropped units; dividing by the keep chance turns the kept ones up, so the layer’s total stays the same on average. \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-layerout}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-layerout)}{\tilde{a}_i}}\) (a-tilde) is what the next layer receives.
\(\displaystyle {\mathbb{E}\big[\htmlClass{eqt sym-layerout}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-layerout)}{\tilde{a}_i}}\big] = \htmlClass{eqt sym-layerout}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-layerout)}{a_i}}}\)
Builds on line 2. Averaged over the coin, the scaled output equals the full output. That is why the full network can run at test time with nothing changed: on average it sends the next layer what training did.
So dropout is a random mask plus one rescale, chosen so the next layer sees the same expected input in training and at test.

The original paper scales the other way: it trains without the rescale and multiplies the outgoing weights by the keep chance at test time. PyTorch’s inverted form rescales during training instead, so the test-time network is untouched. Both give the same expected output. One naming trap: the paper’s \(p\) is the keep chance, one minus PyTorch’s \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{p}}\).

3.1 Dropout in PyTorch

Dropout for one layer of 12 activations; a_tilde is \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-layerout}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-layerout)}{\tilde{a}}}\), and 0.2 is a common rate.

import torch

p = 0.2  # drop chance
a = torch.randn(12)
u = torch.rand_like(a)
m = (u > p).float()  # mask
a_tilde = m * a / (1 - p)  # ã
  1. 1Drop chance \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{p}}\) and the activations \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-layerout}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-layerout)}{a}}\).
  2. 2Mask \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{m}}\), kept with chance one minus \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-prob}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-prob)}{p}}\), line 1.
  3. 3Silence and rescale, line 2.

At inference nothing is dropped: the full network runs, and the training-time rescale already matches its scale. torch.nn.Dropout masks only in training mode; model.train() and model.eval() switch every dropout layer at once.

drop = torch.nn.Dropout(p)
drop.train()
a_train = drop(a)  # masked
drop.eval()
a_test = drop(a)  # equals a
  1. 1Training mode masks and rescales, lines 1 and 2.
  2. 2Evaluation mode passes \(\htmlClass{eqt sym-layerout}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-layerout)}{a}}\) unchanged, line 3.

4 Why it regularizes

Three views explain the same effect.

  • Co-adaptation is broken. A unit that only works when one particular partner fires is useless half the time, so training stops building such pairs and every unit learns something that helps on its own.
  • A large ensemble for the price of one. Each pass trains one of exponentially many thinned networks, all sharing the same weights. The full network at test time, with the rescale of line 2, stands in for the average of all of them (Srivastava et al. 2014). Averaging many models cancels the quirks each one fits.
  • Noise in the hidden layers. Dropout injects noise into the activations, and features that survive small disturbances are the ones that carry over to new data.

The price is slower training: each pass updates a thinned network on a noisy signal, so the loss falls more slowly. The gain is a smaller gap between the training and validation curves (overfitting in one picture). The tool below shows the first view directly.

6 Variants

  • DropConnect zeroes single weights instead of whole units (Wan et al. 2013). It gives a finer-grained mask at a higher cost, since every weight needs a coin.
  • Stochastic depth skips whole residual blocks at random during training and keeps them all at test time (Huang et al. 2016). It regularizes very deep residual networks and shortens training; see CNN Architectures for the residual block.
  • Recurrent dropout uses one mask for all timesteps of a sequence, so a recurrent network’s memory is not scrambled at every step (GRU and LSTM tips).
  • Channel dropout, nn.Dropout1d, zeroes whole channels of a [batch, channel, time] input. For a wristband it mimics a lost electrode, which is why it also appears as an augmentation (Data Augmentation, wearable sensors).
  • Dropout in Transformer and Conformer blocks is applied to each residual branch, and many implementations also drop attention weights. The original Transformer used a rate of 0.1 on each sub-layer output and on the embeddings (Vaswani et al. 2017, section 5.4), and the Conformer applies dropout to each module’s output.

7 Dropout in practice

Put dropout after the activation in fully connected layers, and on the attention weights and residual branches in a Transformer. Settings with their sources: the paper found a retain chance of 0.5 near optimal for hidden units and 0.8 for inputs; AlexNet used dropout with chance 0.5 in its first two fully connected layers (Krizhevsky et al. 2012); Transformers use 0.1.

The paper’s best results on handwritten digits came from dropout together with max-norm, a cap on the size of each unit’s incoming weight vector (Srivastava et al. 2014, section 5.1): dropout lets training take bold steps, and the cap stops the weights growing without bound.

The switch lives on the model, not the layer: model.train() turns every dropout layer on and model.eval() turns them all off. Tune the rate on validation data, starting from the settings above.
