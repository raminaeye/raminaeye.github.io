Guided and Latent Diffusion: Classifier-Free Guidance, Stable Diffusion and DiT

Drawing what the prompt asks for, from guidance to diffusion transformers (DiT).

A text-to-image model adds four things to the denoiser of the earlier pages. It feeds the prompt to the denoiser. It pushes each step toward the prompt, which is guidance. It runs on a compressed copy of the image to save cost, which is latent diffusion and Stable Diffusion (SD); SD v1 and v2 keep the U-Net, the U-shaped denoiser of the training page. For scale, newer models replace that U-Net with a transformer: DiT, then Stable Diffusion 3 (SD3) for text-to-image.

1 The denoiser reads the prompt

Builds on conditional generative adversarial networks (GANs) (Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), section 8), cross-attention (Cross-Attention, section 1), the text encoder (Image-Text Contrastive Learning (CLIP), section 2) and the step-aware U-Net (Training a Diffusion Model: Predicting the Noise, section 5)

The noise guess \(\varepsilon _{\theta }\) of the training page gains an input, the condition \(y\), a class or a prompt: \(\varepsilon_{\theta}(x_{t}, t, y)\), where \(x_{t}\) is the noisy image at step \(t\). A class enters as a learned embedding added to the step embedding. A caption is first turned into token vectors by a frozen text encoder (its weights are not trained further), then read through cross-attention: queries come from the image features and keys and values from the text tokens.

Stable Diffusion v1 reads its prompt with the frozen text encoder of contrastive language-image pretraining (CLIP); Imagen (Saharia et al. 2022) uses a large frozen language model trained only on text and found that a bigger text encoder helped more than a bigger denoiser.

2 Classifier guidance pushes with a second network

Builds on Bayes rule (Probability and Bayes Rule, section 4) and the score (Faster Diffusion Sampling: DDIM and Flow Matching, section 2)

A conditioned denoiser still drifts off the class. Dhariwal and Nichol (2021) trained a classifier on noisy images and used its slope to push each step toward the class. Take one pixel, on the schedule of denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM).

Split the prompted score with Bayes rule, then add the classifier’s slope to the noise guess.
\(\displaystyle \log p(x_{t} | y) =\)
\(\displaystyle \log p(x_{t}) + \log p(y | x_{t})\)
\(\displaystyle - \log p(y)\)
\(p\) is a probability. By Bayes rule in logs, the image given the class is the image alone plus how well a classifier reads the class from it.
\(\displaystyle \nabla \log p(x_{t} | y) =\)
\(\displaystyle \nabla \log p(x_{t}) + \nabla \log p(y | x_{t})\)
\(\nabla\) is the slope in every pixel direction, and \(\nabla \log p\) is the score. Take slopes in \(x_{t}\); \(\log p(y)\) does not depend on the image, so it drops. The prompted score is the plain score plus the classifier’s slope.
\(\displaystyle \tilde{\varepsilon} = \varepsilon _{\theta }(x_{t})\)
\(\displaystyle - s\cdot \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_{t}}\)
\(\displaystyle \cdot \nabla \log p_{\phi }(y | x_{t})\)
\(p_{\phi }\) is the classifier, trained on noisy images. \(s\) is the guidance scale, as in Figure 2. \(\bar\alpha_{t}\) is the clean variance left at step \(t\), and \(\sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_{t}}\) turns a slope into noise units, as in the score. \(\tilde{\varepsilon}\) is the guided guess the sampler uses.
A larger \(s\) gives more class-typical, less varied images.

The catch is the second network, which must be trained on noisy data. Ho and Salimans (2022) note that following its slope “can be interpreted as attempting to confuse an image classifier with a gradient-based adversarial attack”. Classifier-free guidance drops it.

3 Classifier-free guidance pushes with the denoiser itself

Builds on the score and denoising diffusion implicit models (DDIM) (Faster Diffusion Sampling: DDIM and Flow Matching, sections 2 and 3)

During training the prompt is dropped with a chance of 0.1 to 0.2, a dropout of the condition, so one network learns both the prompted and the unprompted guess. At sampling, ask it twice, with the prompt and with the empty condition \(\emptyset\), and step past the prompted guess along their difference; that costs 2 network calls per step, where classifier guidance adds one classifier gradient. \(w\) is the guidance weight, how hard the step follows the prompt. The difference between the two guesses, divided by minus the noise weight, is the slope of a classifier hidden inside the denoiser, an implicit classifier; marker 4 of Figure 3 computes it.

The first term is the guess without the prompt; the bracket is how the prompt changes it. \(w = 0\) ignores the prompt, \(w = 1\) is the plain prompted guess, and \(w \gt 1\) steps past it.
\(\displaystyle \tilde{\varepsilon} = (1 + w')\cdot \varepsilon _{\theta }(x_{t}, y)\)
\(\displaystyle - w'\cdot \varepsilon _{\theta }(x_{t}, \emptyset)\)
\(\displaystyle w' = w - 1\)
Line 2 is the same sum regrouped, as the classifier-free guidance paper writes it; their weight is \(w'\), so their \(w' = 0\) is this page’s \(w = 1\).
The bracket, divided by \(-\sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_{t}}\), is the slope of the log of an implicit classifier, \(\log \frac{p(x_{t} \mid y)}{p(x_{t})}\), so this is classifier guidance with no second network. The paper writes \(z\) and \(c\) for this page’s \(x_{t}\) and \(y\).

Figure 5. At \(w = 0\) any digit; from \(w = 1\) the asked one; far past 1, distorted.

With \(w\) at 3 and a 7 asked for, every sample is a 7.

With no guidance the asked digit often does not appear; from \(w = 1\) up it appears in every sample. Up to moderate guidance every sample is a training digit; at very high guidance the samples lose variety and turn distorted. Stable Diffusion’s default in the diffusers library is \(w = 7.5\) (Hugging Face 2026). On ImageNet, a benchmark of 1.2 million photos in 1,000 classes, classifier-free guidance attains “a trade-off between sample quality and diversity similar to that obtained using classifier guidance”, and stronger guidance has “the expected effect of decreasing sample variety and increasing individual sample fidelity”. Quality there is the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), the distance between feature statistics of real and generated images inside a trained image classifier, lower is better; their best FID came at \(w' = 0.1\) or 0.3.

4 Latent diffusion runs on a small grid

Builds on an encoder and decoder (Masked Image Modeling, section 4), the adversarial loss (Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), section 3), the noise-prediction loss (Training a Diffusion Model: Predicting the Noise, section 1) and the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence (KL Divergence, section 2)

Diffusion on 512x512 pixels spends most of its calls on detail the eye barely sees. Latent diffusion splits the job in two stages: train an autoencoder and freeze it, then train diffusion on its latents (Rombach et al. 2022). The encoder runs once per training image and the decoder once per generated image.

How many times fewer values diffusion has to handle.
\(\displaystyle \text{ratio} = \frac{H\cdot W\cdot 3}{\frac{H}{f}\cdot \frac{W}{f}\cdot c}\)
\(H\) and \(W\) are the image height and width, 3 its colour channels. \(f\) is the downsampling factor and \(c\) the latent channels; Stable Diffusion v1 uses \(f = 8\) and \(c = 4\), so the grid has 64 times fewer cells (8 times fewer per side); each latent cell holds 4 values where a pixel holds 3, so the value count falls by less than the cell count, at any size. The latent diffusion paper tried \(f\) from 1 to 32 and found 4 to 16 “strike a good balance between efficiency and perceptually faithful results”.
The noise-prediction loss of a latent diffusion model (LDM), on the latent, with the prompt read in.
\(\displaystyle L_{\text{LDM}} = E_{\mathcal{E}(x), y, \varepsilon , t}\)
\(\displaystyle \|\varepsilon - \varepsilon _{\theta }(z_{t}, t, \tau _{\theta }(y))\|^{2}\)
\(\mathcal{E}\) is the frozen encoder and \(z_{t}\) is its latent \(\mathcal{E}(x)\), noised to step \(t\). \(\tau _{\theta }\) is the text encoder that turns the prompt into tokens for cross-attention. \(\varepsilon\) is the training noise. E is the average over images, prompts, noise and steps.
It is the noise-prediction loss with \(z\) in place of the pixels.

The autoencoder is trained with a loss on a pretrained network’s features, the adversarial loss (GANs, section 3) of a patch discriminator, a judge that scores each small patch of the image as real or fake, and a small KL term. Stable Diffusion’s autoencoder is a variational autoencoder (VAE; Kingma and Welling 2014) with a very small KL weight. Stable Diffusion v1 multiplies the latent by 0.18215 to bring its spread near 1 (CompVis 2022).

5 Diffusion transformers replace the U-shaped network

Builds on the Vision Transformer (ViT) patches (Masked Image Modeling, section 2), LayerNorm (Normalization Layers, section 2.2), adaptive instance normalization (AdaIN) (Training GANs, section 7), attention’s cost (Transformer Cost and Efficiency, section 1) and the multilayer perceptron (MLP) (Multilayer Perceptrons (MLP), section 2)

A DiT (Peebles and Xie 2023) cuts the latent into patches, marker 1 of Figure 10, and runs a transformer on them as tokens. Patch size sets the token count, and attention’s cost grows with its square.

The step and the class enter through adaptive LayerNorm with a zero start (adaLN-Zero): a small MLP turns their summed embeddings (marker 3) into a scale and shift for each LayerNorm, the StyleGAN idea, plus a gate \(g\) on each block’s output (marker 4) that starts at zero, so every block (marker 2) starts by passing its input through unchanged. The last block gives the noise guess (marker 5). On class-conditional ImageNet at 256x256 with classifier-free guidance, the extra-large (XL) model with patch size 2, DiT-XL/2, reached an FID of 2.27, and FID kept falling as compute grew.

Stable Diffusion 3 uses a multimodal DiT (MMDiT) that runs text and image tokens through joint attention (one attention over the text and image tokens together), trained with rectified flow (Esser et al. 2024). The same denoising recipe also generates audio in DiffWave (Kong et al. 2021), a speech synthesizer, which denoises the raw waveform in place of pixels, all audio samples together rather than one after another, with a dilated convolutional network (filters with gaps, so each layer sees farther along the sound) rather than a transformer.

6 One guided step in PyTorch

Builds on the DDIM step (Faster Diffusion Sampling: DDIM and Flow Matching, section 3)

One guided step on a latent batch, with a one-layer class-conditioned network standing in for Stable Diffusion’s U-Net.

import torch
import torch.nn as nn

alphas_cumprod = torch.cumprod(1 - torch.linspace(1e-4, 0.02, 1000), dim=0)
conv = nn.Conv2d(4, 4, 3, padding=1)
emb = nn.Embedding(11, 4)  # 10 classes, and index 10 is the empty one
model = lambda x, t, y: conv(x) + emb(y).view(-1, 4, 1, 1)

w, empty = 7.5, 10
x = torch.randn(2, 4, 64, 64)  # a latent batch of 2
y = torch.tensor([7, 3])
a_t, a_next = alphas_cumprod[999], alphas_cumprod[979]

with torch.no_grad():
    y_in = torch.cat([y, torch.full_like(y, empty)])
    eps_both = model(torch.cat([x, x]), 999, y_in)
    eps_cond, eps_uncond = eps_both.chunk(2)
    eps = eps_uncond + w * (eps_cond - eps_uncond)
    x0_hat = (x - (1 - a_t).sqrt() * eps) / a_t.sqrt()
    x = a_next.sqrt() * x0_hat + (1 - a_next).sqrt() * eps
  1. 1The training schedule, a stand-in network whose last class index is the empty condition \(\emptyset\), \(w = 7.5\), a batch of two latents with their classes, and \(\bar\alpha\) at this step and the next kept one.
  2. 2The batch twice, once with the conditions and once with \(\emptyset\).
  3. 3One network call for both halves, so guidance doubles the compute per step.
  4. 4Split the guesses and combine them as in classifier-free guidance.
  5. 5The DDIM update with \(\eta = 0\) and the guided guess; after the last step the latent is divided by 0.18215 and decoded.

In diffusers, guidance_scale is \(w\), “enabled when guidance_scale > 1”, and negative_prompt replaces \(\emptyset\) with text to steer away from.

Over-saturated, unnatural colours\(w\) too high, so the guided guess pushes pixels past the training range; Imagen found high weights give “highly saturated and unnatural images”lower \(w\); Imagen clips each step’s clean-image guess with dynamic thresholding, which clips to a high percentile of that image’s own pixel values and rescales, instead of a fixed [−1, 1]
