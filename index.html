The classics that still run the world. How much can you learn without a single GPU?

  • Maximum Likelihood EstimationFitting a model to data by picking the numbers that make the data most likely.
  • The EM AlgorithmWhen part of the data is hidden, guess the labels, refit, and repeat.

  • Logistic RegressionA line that says yes or no. The sigmoid, an S-shaped curve that squeezes any score into the range from 0 to 1, turns a score into a probability, and 0.5 draws the boundary.
  • Multilayer Perceptrons (MLP)When a line is not enough. Its layers each mix the numbers they receive with learned weights and bend the mix, so stacked layers can bend the boundary too. One dial grows the model from a straight boundary to a smooth curve to a crazy overfit, where the model learns the training examples so closely that it does worse on new ones.
  • Decision TreesTwenty questions on your features. Pick the purest split, grow, prune before it memorizes.
  • Random ForestsMany trees on resampled data vote, and the windows each tree never saw test it for free.
  • Gradient BoostingSmall trees added one at a time, each fitting what is still wrong: gradient descent with trees.
  • XGBoost and LightGBMCurvature-aware leaf scores, a price on every leaf, and split search fast enough for millions of rows.
  • Support Vector Machines (SVM)Maximum margin (the widest empty gap you can leave between the boundary and the nearest points on each side). Hinge loss (the penalty for points on the wrong side of the margin), support vectors (the borderline points that hold the margin up), and the C knob that trades margin width against violations.
  • k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN)Ask the neighbors. No training, just voting, and one slider that shows bias (oversmoothing the pattern away) versus variance (twitching at every noisy neighbor) live.
  • Naive BayesClassifying by counting. Each word moves the class bars, and smoothing (adding a small count to everything) stops zero counts from zeroing everything.
  • k-Means ClusteringFind the clumps with no labels. Two steps repeated: points pick the nearest center, centers jump to the middle.
  • Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM)k-means gives every point one cluster and every cluster a circle.
  • Hidden Markov Models (HMM)A Markov chain is a state that hops by fixed odds.
  • Principal Component Analysis (PCA)Faces from parts. The notebook's eigenfaces, the ghostly basic face patterns that get mixed in different amounts to build a face, rebuilt in your browser: one slider from ghost to person.
  • Independent Component Analysis (ICA)Two voices, two microphones. How ICA unmixes recordings into independent sources: whitening (the PCA step), the non-Gaussian objective, FastICA one update at a time, and why a real room's echoes break it.
  • Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF)A spectrogram rebuilt as sound templates times when each one plays. Why non-negative parts add up, the two costs, the multiplicative updates, choosing the rank, and how a convolutive version pulls room echo out of a recording.

Transformers are models built by stacking attention blocks. Attention is weighted averaging: each position looks at the others, weighs how much each one matters, and averages their information with those weights. So why does the bill come out quadratic (growing with the square of the length, so doubling the input makes the work four times as large)?

  • AttentionAttention is weighted averaging with learned weights: each token scores every other token, turns the scores into weights, and blends what they carry. Several heads do it in parallel.
  • Positional Encoding: Sinusoidal, Learned and ALiBiAttention sees a bag of tokens. Fixed waves, a learned table, or a distance penalty tell it where each token sits, and only one of them stretches to longer inputs.
  • Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)Turn each query and key by its position, and the score depends only on how far apart the two tokens are.
  • Attention Masks: Padding, Causal and Loss MaskingA mask is a matrix added to the scores before the softmax: zero where a pair may attend, negative infinity where it must not.
  • The Transformer BlockAttention mixes, a small feed-forward network refines, residuals carry everything up. Stack the block and you get BERT, the translation model, or GPT.
  • Cross-AttentionQueries from one stream, keys and values from another: a translation reading its source sentence, or a fixed set of learned queries reading an input of any size.
  • Transformer Cost and EfficiencyWhy attention's cost grows with the square of the length, where that cost lands in training and generation, and a map of the five techniques that cut it.
  • FlashAttentionThe same exact attention, computed in tiles that stay in the GPU's fast memory, so the n by n score table never travels to slow memory.
  • The KV CacheWhy generation stores every past key and value, what that saves in compute and what it costs in memory.
  • Multi-Query and Grouped-Query AttentionQuery heads share stored key and value heads, shrinking the cache in proportion with little loss in quality.
  • Sliding-Window AttentionEach token reads only a recent window; stacked layers still reach far, a rolling buffer caps the cache, and attention sinks keep long streams stable.
  • Attention Sinks and Streaming GenerationA full-attention model collapses when its cache is cut to a window; keeping the first few tokens, the attention sinks, lets it stream for millions of tokens in fixed memory.
  • Bottleneck Adapters and CompacterFreeze the model, train two small modules per layer, and serve every task from one copy.
  • LoRA and QLoRATrain a small low-rank change to the frozen weights, then fold it away.
  • The Conformer BlockHow a speech encoder adds a convolution module between two half feed-forwards so one block hears both short sounds and the whole sentence, what the paper's ablation shows, and how Branchformer and Squeezeformer rewired it.
  • Conformer in PyTorch: Training and StreamingThe Conformer block written as PyTorch classes, trained with CTC and a warm-up schedule, then run on live audio with a chunked attention mask at a chosen latency.

One model, many tasks. When the tasks fight, whose gradient (the arrow that points where its task's loss rises fastest; training steps the other way) wins?

  • MGDA (Multiple Gradient Descent Algorithm)Blend the task gradients into their shortest mix, every weight zero or more and all adding to one, and step against it.
  • GradNorm (Gradient Normalization)Learn one loss weight per task (the number its loss is multiplied by) so every task trains at the same pace.

I studied at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign and began my career in audio machine learning, with internships at the Advanced Digital Sciences Center in Singapore and at Adobe. At Dolby Laboratories I worked on deep learning and audio codecs. As the first hire at CurieAI, I helped develop models that detect respiratory symptoms from noisy audio and scaled the technology to hundreds of patients.

I then moved into natural language processing. At UnitedHealth Group I was the first ML lead on the team and built natural language search for health benefits, which became the foundation of an enterprise-wide semantic search platform. As ML lead for the enterprise team, I trained language models from scratch, grew the team to more than 50 people, and brought my background in sensory signals to how the company applied machine learning to search.

At Precision Neuroscience I applied signal processing and machine learning to brain-computer interfaces, translating brain signals into real-time actions. I am now an AI research scientist at Meta, working on multitask models for sensory data.
