Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

Two rackets, one ball, and nobody told either of them the other one is learning.

Here is a question that breaks everything single-agent RL assumes: what do you do when your opponent is learning too?

Up to now the world always sat still while the agent learned. The bananas never adapted. The reacher arm's target never fought back. You could collect experience on Monday and it was still true on Friday.

Tennis ruins that. Two rackets, each one learning, each one's world containing the other. Your racket gets better, so rallies get longer, so the other racket sees longer rallies, so it learns faster, so your world changes again. The ground moves under both of them the whole time. That moving ground is what makes multi-agent RL its own beast, not just single-agent times two.

This one is my TennisGym repo: Unity ML-Agents tennis, two rackets, one ball, and one DDPG agent per racket.

The setup

Each racket sees 8 numbers: positions and velocities of the ball and the rackets. Each outputs 2 continuous numbers: scoot toward the ball, and jump. Continuous actions, so this is actor-critic territory again, same as the reacher.

The rewards are delightfully lopsided: +0.1 every time you clear the net, -0.01 when you let it drop or sail it out of bounds. The environment counts as solved when a racket averages at least 0.5 over 100 episodes, taking the better of the two rackets. My run crossed 0.5 around episode 50, settled near 2.0, and peaked around 2.5.

One DDPG agent per racket

The method is almost embarrassingly simple: give each racket its own DDPG agent, an actor and a critic each, and train both at the same time. No special multi-agent machinery. Each agent treats the other racket as part of the environment, which is technically a lie, since the other racket is learning, and it works anyway.

Same DDPG recipe as the reacher page: the actor proposes continuous actions, the critic judges them, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise keeps exploration going, and a big replay buffer smooths the updates. Here are my real numbers from the repo:

Plus the usual stabilizers: batch norm in both networks, gradient clipping on both, and soft target updates. I also tried a fancier prioritized replay buffer and an ensemble of agents voting on actions. Neither helped enough to justify the complexity, so the plain version is what shipped.

The gotcha: your diary is lying to you

A replay buffer is a diary: it assumes the past predicts the future. In tennis, your diary is full of rallies against last week's opponent, who was dumber. Training on stale experience is like studying your rival's old matches: useful right up until they change their game, which they do every single episode.

This is the thing people miss about multi-agent RL. It is not just single-agent times two. The non-stationarity, the fancy word for "the world keeps moving", breaks the core assumption every replay buffer is built on. My run's jagged curve is that struggle, drawn in blue.
