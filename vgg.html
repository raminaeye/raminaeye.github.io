1 Big picture

AlexNet mixed kernel sizes and layer widths, so nobody knew how much depth itself mattered. Simonyan and Zisserman held everything else fixed [1]: 3×3 convolutions with stride 1 everywhere (except three 1×1 layers in configuration C), five max-pool stages that halve the image, channels doubling from 64 to 512 across the stages, and three fully connected layers. Only depth varied, in configurations A to E, from 11 to 19 weight layers.

VGG is a plain feed-forward stack; nothing carries over between batches. Today it is historical as a classifier, but its pretrained conv features live on as the fixed network inside perceptual losses for style transfer and super-resolution [2].

2 Why 3×3 stacks

Three stacked 3×3 layers see a 7×7 patch of their input, as far as one 7×7 layer (the reach picture is on Receptive Fields). Compare their weights, with \(C\) channels in and out of every layer:

Weights of a three-layer 3×3 stack and of one 7×7 layer with the same reach.
\(\displaystyle {W_{3\times3\ \text{stack}} = 3\,\big(3^2\,\htmlClass{eqt sym-var}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-var)}{C}}^2\big)}\)
A 3×3 layer gives every pair of input and output channels its own 3×3 grid of weights, so it holds \(3^2 C^2\); the stack has three such layers.
\(\displaystyle {W_{7\times7} = 7^2\,\htmlClass{eqt sym-var}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-var)}{C}}^2}\)
One 7×7 layer gives every channel pair a 7×7 grid.
Both grow with \(C^2\), so the comparison holds at any width: the stack sees as far as the single 7×7 layer with fewer weights, and puts two extra ReLUs between.

3 How deep is enough

The paper trained configurations A to E on ImageNet from the same parts and tested each at a single scale, with images resized so their shorter side is 256 pixels. A-LRN is A plus local response normalization; C is B plus three 1×1 layers; D puts 3×3 layers in those places; E adds a fourth 3×3 layer to the last three stages.

Error falls from A to D and levels off at E; in the paper's words, "the error rate of our architecture saturates when the depth reaches 19 layers". At equal depth the all-3×3 D beats C. A-LRN was no better than A, so LRN was dropped from the deeper nets. The ILSVRC-2014 submission was an ensemble of 7 models at 7.3% top-5 test error, and 6.8% with 2 models after submission.

5 Where the compute, weights and memory go

Through the first four stages, halving the image while doubling the channels keeps each conv layer's compute about level from stage to stage. The last stage keeps 512 channels while the image halves again, so its layers cost a quarter of a stage-4 layer. Weights grow with the channels squared, and most of VGG-16's roughly 138 million (the paper's Table 2) sit in the first fully connected layer. Activation memory is largest in the first stages, where the image is biggest.

7 In practice

torchvision.models.vgg16 and vgg16_bn, the variant with BatchNorm after each conv, load pretrained weights [4]. Perceptual losses compare images through the frozen conv stack's activations [2].

def make_stage(c_in, c_out, n):
    layers = []
    for i in range(n):                      # n 3x3 convs, padding keeps H and W
        layers += [nn.Conv2d(c_in if i == 0 else c_out, c_out, 3, padding=1), nn.ReLU(inplace=True)]
    layers.append(nn.MaxPool2d(2, 2))       # halves H and W
    return nn.Sequential(*layers)
vgg16_features = nn.Sequential(             # (N, 3, 224, 224)
    make_stage(3, 64, 2),                   # -> (N, 64, 112, 112)
    make_stage(64, 128, 2),                 # -> (N, 128, 56, 56)
    make_stage(128, 256, 3),                # -> (N, 256, 28, 28)
    make_stage(256, 512, 3),                # -> (N, 512, 14, 14)
    make_stage(512, 512, 3),                # -> (N, 512, 7, 7)
)
head_in = 512 * 7 * 7                       # the first fully connected layer's input
  1. 1One stage: \(n\) 3×3 conv and ReLU layers, then a pool.
  2. 2VGG-16 as five calls with the paper's stage widths.

VGG got deeper by stacking the same block. Can a network get wider as well without its compute growing? Inception tried that.
