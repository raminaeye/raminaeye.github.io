Short, hands-on tutorials on the ideas I keep coming back to. Each one starts with intuition, walks through the math in plain words, and most have something you can play with. The code behind them lives in my model-optimization repo.

Classical Machine Learning

Linear Regression

Fitting a line by walking downhill. Gradient descent versus the closed-form answer, and what learning rate does to the walk.

Logistic Regression

A line that says yes or no. The sigmoid turns a score into a probability, and 0.5 draws the boundary.

Neural Nets (MLP)

When a line is not enough. One dial grows the model from a straight boundary to a smooth curve to a crazy overfit.

Trees, Forests, Boosting

Twenty questions, then a forest. One tree memorizes, many vote, and boosting fits the last round's mistakes.

Support Vector Machines

The widest street. Hinge loss, margins, and the C knob that trades street width against violations.

k-Nearest Neighbors

Ask the neighbors. No training, just voting, and one slider that shows bias versus variance live.

Naive Bayes

Classifying by counting. Each word moves the class bars, and smoothing stops zero counts from zeroing everything.

k-Means

Find the clumps with no labels. Two steps repeated: points pick the nearest center, centers jump to the middle.

PCA

Faces from parts. The notebook's eigenfaces, rebuilt in your browser: one slider from ghost to person.

t-SNE

PCA's nonlinear follow-up. Neighbor probabilities, the heavy-tailed t, and the perplexity knob that remakes the map.

Recommenders

People who liked this. Taste as geometry: same-taste users versus same-content movies, side by side.

Deep Learning Fundamentals

Convolutional Neural Networks

How convolutions actually work, what the receptive field is doing, and why stacked small kernels beat one big one.

RNNs, GRUs, and LSTMs

Sequences and memory, why gradients vanish on the way back, and the gates that fix it. The GRU is a linear highway for memory.

Loss Functions

You know me, I am gonna talk about loss functions. BCE, cross entropy, and CTC, with every equation read out loud.

Errors and Scaling

Bias, variance, and the errors you cannot train away. Know your floor before you blame your model.

Transformers & LLMs

Transformers

Attention is just weighted averaging with learned weights. Masks, multihead, KV caches, and the quadratic bill, all on toy examples.

Adapters and LoRA

Fine-tune a giant model while training almost nothing. Rank is a dial, not a magic number.

Self-Supervised Learning

Teach a model without labels. SimCLR, BYOL, and what the temperature is actually doing to the loss.

Model Efficiency

Quantization

The lazy version of quantization, and that is not a bad thing. Fewer bits, same model, almost free.

Neural Architecture Search

Let the machine design the model. The search space matters more than the search, so build it carefully.

Knowledge Distillation

Shrink a giant model into a small one without much performance loss. The teacher's soft labels do the teaching.

Model Pruning

Cut the weights that do not matter and keep the accuracy. Sparsity is free compression if you do it right.

Audio & Signals

The Conformer

The speech model that hears both the phoneme and the sentence. Convolution plus attention, macaron style.

ADC Fundamentals

If analog input is a ramp, ADC is a staircase. Bits, noise, and the 6 dB rule, made playable.
