Audio & Signals Basics

The path a signal takes, and two ways to look at it.

A sound reaches a model through a chain. A microphone turns air pressure into a voltage, an amplifier makes it larger, an ADC turns the voltage into numbers, filters and transforms reshape those numbers, denoising takes the noise out, and feature extraction boils each moment down to the few numbers a model reads. Each page in this series covers one stage of that chain, and every stage can be judged in two domains, time and frequency.

1 Two ways to look at one signal

A signal has two standard descriptions. The time domain shows when things happen; the frequency domain shows which frequencies are present. Some things are obvious in one domain and hidden in the other.

The clip on this page is sampled 8,000 times per second (a sampling rate fs of 8 kHz). It holds a three-note chord (220, 277 and 330 Hz) and one sharp click. Levels are in decibels (dB), a log scale where every 20 dB down is ten times smaller in amplitude.

The Fourier transform moves a signal between the two domains; it is built from scratch on Fourier Transform and the FFT.

2 One signal down the chain

On the way in, the microphone also picks up faint hiss and a high whistle at 5.2 kHz. The ADC (analog-to-digital converter) then samples at 8 kHz, and samples taken that often can only hold frequencies up to half the rate, 4 kHz, the Nyquist frequency. So an anti-alias filter must remove the whistle first; Aliasing and Anti-Aliasing Filters shows what happens otherwise.

The amplifier and the filter work in the time view; the spectrum and the denoiser work in the frequency view. Most of this series is about moving back and forth between the two.

4 Where signals go wrong

Each stage has one classic failure. When you see the symptom, the linked page explains it.

What you seeLikely causeWhat to do
Peaks with flat topsThe input went past the ADC's range (clipping)Lower the gain; see ADC Fundamentals
Tones that were never in the roomSampling too slowly (aliasing)Filter before sampling; see Aliasing and Anti-Aliasing Filters
One tone smeared over many frequenciesA frame cut off abruptly, with no window to fade its edges (leakage)Taper each frame; see The STFT and Spectrograms
A click that arrives late or bent after filteringPhase and delay of the filterUse linear phase or zero phase; see How FIR and IIR Filters Shape Magnitude and Phase
Short, isolated tones left after denoisingMusical noiseSubtract more than the noise estimate, or keep a minimum gain; see Spectral Subtraction for Denoising

How does a continuous voltage become a list of numbers in the first place? That is ADC Fundamentals.
