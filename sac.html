A network that sets a robot's turning forces keeps a range of choices and is rewarded for staying undecided.

1 Why commit to one set of forces

1.1 Fixed noise is one more setting to tune

Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) commits to one set of turning forces, so it needs noise added from outside to try new ones. On a real robot, every wild move costs time and can damage it.

Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3) commits and explores the same way. So the learner should decide for itself how far to stray from its best forces.

Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) lets the network that picks the forces keep a range instead. That range lets it explore without noise added from outside.

The agent is the learner that picks the moves. (RL Basics) A state is the agent's situation right now. A move, or action, is one choice the agent can make in a state. A reward is the number the world hands back after each move, good or bad. (RL Basics)

On a robot, sensing, moving and scoring repeat many times every second.

The running example is the Reacher task: a robot arm with two joints whose actions are four torques. A torque is a turning force a motor applies at a joint. On the Reacher each torque is a number from -1 to 1. Each joint can turn about two axes, so each joint gets two torques. Every step the arm senses its state, numbers describing the position, rotation and speed of its parts, and earns a small reward whenever its hand is inside a moving goal. (Continuous-Action RL)

The return is all the reward still to come, with later rewards counted a little less. (RL Basics) A move's value \(Q(s,a)\), also called its score, is the return the agent expects if it makes move \(a\) in state \(s\) and plays on from there. (RL Basics) So the hand must follow the goal as it moves, not just reach it once.

DDPG and TD3 learn two kinds of network. The deterministic actor \(\mu_\theta\) is a network with weights \(\theta\) that outputs one action every time, with no random draw. (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG): Actor and Critic) The critic \(Q_\phi\) is a network with weights \(\phi\) that scores a state and an action together; its score stands for the return the agent expects. (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG): Actor and Critic) Both networks learn from the steps the arm has already taken.

DDPG and TD3 explore by adding noise to that one set of torques. Exploration noise is a small random nudge added to the actor's actions while it learns, so the agent tries moves it is not sure about. (Training DDPG: Target Networks, Replay and Noise) On a real robot every one of those random tries costs time and wear.

1.2 Every step costs on a real robot

The Minitaur is a small four-legged robot with eight motors, two per leg. Its actions are each leg's swing angle and extension, which a low-level controller turns into motor positions. Each fall costs time and can damage it, so the learner must reuse old steps and explore without wild moves.

2 A range of torques instead of one torque

A policy is the agent's rule for choosing a move in each state; it may always pick the same move or pick by chance. (RL Basics) SAC's actor draws its torques from a bell.

A Gaussian actor outputs one centre and one width per torque and draws each torque from that bell. We write SAC's actor as \(\pi_\theta\), with weights \(\theta\), because it draws its torques by chance; \(\mu_\theta\) is the actor that outputs one action every time. For the Reacher that is one centre and one width per torque. The actor learns all these numbers itself, so the width is not a noise size set by hand.

The tanh function squashes any number into -1 to 1, the torque range. (Continuous-Action RL) Each draw is then squashed, so every torque stays in range.

Entropy \(\mathcal{H}\) is how undecided the policy is; for a bell of torques, it is how wide the bell is. (REINFORCE and the Baseline) Staying undecided keeps several good ways of moving alive, so the actor does not lock onto one fragile gait (a way of walking) too early. The entropy bonus is a small extra term that rewards the policy for staying undecided between moves, so it keeps trying the others for longer. (REINFORCE and the Baseline) SAC adds this bonus to each step's reward, and a wider bell earns more of it, so the critic's score also counts the bonus still to come.

The critic learns by being pulled toward a target, the step's reward plus its own score for what comes next, counted a little less. (Continuous-Action RL) That target moves whenever the critic moves. SAC also keeps TD3's two critics but not its other two changes.

The temperature \(\alpha\) sets how much staying undecided is worth against reward. A higher temperature keeps the bell wider.

3 SAC's objective, target, actor and temperature

3.1 Reward plus how undecided the policy is

Replay keeps a memory of past steps, and training draws random batches from it. (Deep Q-Networks (DQN)) The sign \(\sim\) means drawn at random from. (Common Probability Distributions) \(\log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)\) is the log of how likely the actor was to draw this action from its bell, the height of the bell at that action; very negative means an unlikely draw, which happens more often when the bell is wide. (Policy Gradient Methods) SAC scores each draw's unlikeliness as its bonus for staying undecided.

Equation 1. Reward plus how undecided the policy is. The Reacher arm earns reward for keeping its hand on the goal and also a small bonus at every step where its torques stay varied rather than locked to one value.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#0e6b59}{J}(\htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\pi_\theta}) =\)
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\sum_{t}}\ \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\, \htmlStyle{color:#0e6b59}{r_{t+1}} + \htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{\alpha}\,\htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{\mathcal{H}_t} \big]\)
A policy's score is reward plus a bonus for staying undecided; the actor should succeed while staying as undecided as it can afford. \(J(\pi_\theta)\) is the score the actor is trained to raise; on SAC it counts the entropy bonus on top of reward. \(r_{t+1}\) is the reward that follows the step-t action. \(\mathcal{H}_t\) is the entropy in state \(s_t\), how undecided the policy is there. \(\alpha\) is the temperature. The average \(\mathbb{E}\) runs over the states the actor visits and the torques it draws, and the sum runs over the steps of an episode. An episode is one run from the start until it ends. (RL Basics) With \(\alpha\) at zero this is the sum of rewards; the paper adds the discount in practice. The discount \(\gamma\) sets how much a reward one step later still counts. (RL Basics)
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{h} = -\log \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\pi_\theta}(\htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{a} \mid \htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{s_t})\)
\(h\) is how unlikely one draw was: minus the log-probability of the torques the actor drew.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{\mathcal{H}_t} = \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi_\theta}}[\htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{h}]\)
The entropy is the average of how unlikely the actor's own draws are: unlikely draws on average mean a wide bell. \(a \sim \pi_\theta\) means torques drawn from the actor's whole bell. Line 3 defines the bonus used in line 1. This builds on the return (RL Basics), the entropy bonus (REINFORCE and the Baseline), the stochastic policy (RL Basics) and the log-probability (Policy Gradient Methods).

SAC never sums this directly; the target below builds it one step at a time.

3.2 A target that rewards staying undecided

A slow copy of a network is a copy updated only a little at a time. In DDPG both the actor and the critic have a slow copy, marked by a prime on the weights, and the targets are built from those copies. (Training DDPG: Target Networks, Replay and Noise) In DDPG both copies enter the critic's target; SAC keeps only the slow critics. SAC does not need a slow actor, because its next torques come from a fresh draw.

The live networks are the actor and critic being trained, with weights \(\theta\) and \(\phi\). (Training DDPG: Target Networks, Replay and Noise) The soft update blends a small share \(\tau\) of the live weights into each slow copy after every update, instead of overwriting it. (Deep Q-Networks (DQN)) Soft in Soft Actor-Critic means a move's value counts the entropy bonus; it is unrelated to the soft update that blends slow copies. Every slow critic below is blended this way after each update.

The DDPG critic's target is the step's reward plus the discounted score the slow critic gives to the slow actor's next torques. (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG): Actor and Critic) TD3 trains two critics side by side and builds the target from the lower of their two scores. (Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3)) SAC's target adds one more term to that lower score.

Equation 2. A target that rewards staying undecided. For a stored arm step the actor draws next torques; the target adds their lower slow-critic score and a bonus that is bigger when that draw was unlikely, which on average means a wide bell.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{a'} \sim \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\pi_\theta}(\cdot \mid \htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{s'})\)
\(a'\) is the next torques drawn from the live actor. The dot means "over every possible torque": \(\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid s')\) is the whole bell in state \(s'\).
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#8e44ad}{q'} = \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\min_{i=1,2}}\ \htmlStyle{color:#8e44ad}{Q_{\phi'_i}}(\htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{s'}, \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{a'})\)
\(q'\) is the lower slow-critic score as in TD3. (Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3))
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{h'} = -\log \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\pi_\theta}(\htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{a'} \mid \htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{s'})\)
\(h'\) is how unlikely that one draw was, one sample of Equation 1 line 3.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#0e6b59}{y} = \htmlStyle{color:#0e6b59}{r} + \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\gamma}\,\big(\htmlStyle{color:#8e44ad}{q'} + \htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{\alpha}\,\htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{h'}\big)\)
TD3's target, with the slow actor replaced by a fresh draw from the live actor and the future entropy counted as part of the value. The bonus must measure how undecided the live actor is, so the next torques are drawn from its own bell; the SAC paper found a slow actor unnecessary. \(r\) is the reward stored with this step, \(\gamma\) and \(s'\) are as in the DDPG critic's target, and \(\alpha h'\) is the entropy bonus for that draw. (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG): Actor and Critic) Line 4 is Equation 1 written one step at a time. This builds on TD3's lower-of-two target and Equation 1.

Both live critics then move toward y by shrinking the squared gap between their score and y.

3.3 The actor's draw and loss

The DDPG actor update moves the actor's weights so its torques shift the way the critic's score rises. (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG): Actor and Critic) A torque picked by chance has no smooth rule linking it to the actor's weights, so the critic's slope cannot pass back through it. The reparameterization trick writes a random draw as the centre plus the width times a fixed random number, so the critic's slope can flow back into the actor. The draw is then squashed into the torque range.

Equation 3. The actor's draw. For each of the arm's four torques the actor outputs a centre and a width; a standard random draw is scaled by the width, shifted by the centre and squashed into -1 to 1.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{u} = \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{m_\theta(s)} + \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\sigma_\theta(s)}\,\htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\xi}\)
Move the randomness into a fixed input, so the torques become a smooth function of the actor's weights and the critic's slope can flow back into them, as in DDPG. \(m_\theta(s)\) is the centre, one per torque. \(\sigma_\theta(s)\) is the width, one per torque, the bell width. \(\xi\) is a fresh random number drawn from a standard normal bell, one per torque, drawn independently. (Common Probability Distributions) \(u\) is the unsquashed draw.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{a} = \tanh(\htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{u})\)
\(\tanh\) squashes into the torque range. Equation 4 passes slopes through these two lines. This builds on the DDPG actor, the DDPG actor update and the Gaussian bell. (Common Probability Distributions)
Equation 4. The actor's loss. For each stored arm state, the actor's drawn torques should score high on the lower live critic, and the actor is penalised when its draws are too predictable.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#8e44ad}{q} = \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\min_{i=1,2}}\ \htmlStyle{color:#8e44ad}{Q_{\phi_i}}(\htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{s}, \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{a})\)
\(q\) is the lower of the two live critics' scores for the drawn torques \(a\) from Equation 3.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{L(\theta)} =\)
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\mathbb{E}_{s,\xi}}\big[\htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{\alpha} \log \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\pi_\theta}(\htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{a} \mid \htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{s}) - \htmlStyle{color:#8e44ad}{q}\big]\)
Climb the critic like DDPG, but each bit of indecision given up costs \(\alpha\). \(\alpha \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)\) is large when the draw was very likely, meaning a narrow bell, so minimising it keeps the bell wide. Minus \(q\) is "climb the lower critic". The average runs over stored states and fresh draws \(\xi\). Equation 1 per state, as a loss to minimise. This builds on the DDPG actor update, TD3's lower of two, and Equations 1 and 3.

So the actor climbs the lower critic while keeping its bell from narrowing.

3.4 Tuning the temperature automatically

SAC can also set the temperature itself while it trains; this is called automatic tuning. The target entropy \(\bar{\mathcal{H}}\) is how undecided automatic tuning holds the policy to. Tuning raises the temperature when the actor grows too decided and lowers it when the actor grows too undecided.

Equation 5. Tuning the temperature. When the arm's torques are more varied than the target entropy asks for, alpha falls; when they narrow below it, alpha rises, and the actor's next update widens the bell again.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{h} = -\log \htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{\pi_\theta}(\htmlStyle{color:#a04000}{a} \mid \htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{s})\)
\(h\) is one sample of how undecided the actor is, with \(a\) drawn from the actor, as \(h'\) in Equation 2.
\(\displaystyle \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{L(\alpha)} = \htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{\alpha}\,\htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{h} - \htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{\alpha}\,\htmlStyle{color:#7d6608}{\bar{\mathcal{H}}}\big]\)
Choose how undecided the actor should stay, and let \(\alpha\) move to hold it there; \(\alpha\) rises when the actor is more decided than the target entropy asks for and falls when it is less. \(\bar{\mathcal{H}}\) is the target entropy. This loss is never minimised to the end; it only supplies a slope for \(\alpha\). The slope is the gap \(h\) minus the target entropy, so each step nudges \(\alpha\) up when the actor is too decided and down when it is too undecided. The actor's next update (Equation 4) widens or narrows its bell in response, and that is what closes the gap. This replaces hand-tuning \(\alpha\) in Equation 1; the code's alpha_loss is this line with log alpha in place of alpha. This builds on Equation 1 line 3 and Equation 4.

The SAC papers write \(\phi\) for the actor, \(\theta\) for the critics, a bar for slow copies, \(\epsilon\) for the random draw, and \(r(s_t, a_t)\) for the reward; we keep the series letters and write \(r_{t+1}\).

5 SAC's actor and update in PyTorch

The five equations above become two code blocks below: the actor, then one update step.

class SquashedGaussianActor(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, state_dim, act_dim):
        super().__init__()
        self.net = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(state_dim, HIDDEN), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(HIDDEN, HIDDEN), nn.ReLU())
        self.mean = nn.Linear(HIDDEN, act_dim)
        self.log_std = nn.Linear(HIDDEN, act_dim)

    def forward(self, s):
        h = self.net(s)
        return self.mean(h), self.log_std(h)

def sample_action(actor, s):
    m, log_std = actor(s)
    std = log_std.exp()
    u = m + std * torch.randn_like(m)
    a = torch.tanh(u)
    logp = Normal(m, std).log_prob(u).sum(-1) - torch.log(1 - a.pow(2) + EPS_GUARD).sum(-1)
    return a, logp
  1. 1First, the network: it returns one centre and one log width per torque. The network outputs the log of the width, so the width stays positive.
  2. 2Next, the draw of Equation 3: a fresh random draw is scaled by the width, shifted by the centre and squashed into the torque range.
  3. 3Last, the log-probability of the squashed draw from Equation 3. Squashing piles torques up near -1 and 1, so the likelihood of a squashed torque is corrected by the second term. A tiny guard constant is added that avoids the log of zero.

One update step uses both blocks.

def sac_update(batch, actor, q1, q2, q1_t, q2_t, log_alpha, target_entropy):
    s, a, r, s2 = batch
    alpha = log_alpha.exp()
    # the target
    with torch.no_grad():
        a2, logp2 = sample_action(actor, s2)
        target_q = torch.min(q1_t(s2, a2), q2_t(s2, a2)) - alpha * logp2
        y = r + gamma * target_q
    # both critics toward the shared target
    critic_loss = mse(q1(s, a), y) + mse(q2(s, a), y)
    critic_loss.backward()
    critic_opt.step()
    # the actor loss and the temperature loss
    a_new, logp = sample_action(actor, s)
    actor_loss = (alpha * logp - torch.min(q1(s, a_new), q2(s, a_new))).mean()
    actor_loss.backward()
    actor_opt.step()
    alpha_loss = -(log_alpha * (logp + target_entropy).detach()).mean()
    alpha_loss.backward()
    alpha_opt.step()
    alpha = log_alpha.exp()
    # the slow copies blend a small step
    soft_update(q1_t, q1, tau)
    soft_update(q2_t, q2, tau)
  1. 1First, the target of Equation 2: under torch.no_grad() the target is a fixed number, not trained. The target is the reward plus the discount times the lower slow score minus the temperature times the log-probability of the next draw. Both live critics move toward the one shared target. mse is the mean squared gap, as Equation 2 shows.
  2. 2Next, the actor loss of Equation 4 and the temperature loss of Equation 5: the actor loss climbs the lower live critic while keeping the bell wide. The temperature loss uses log alpha in place of alpha, which keeps alpha positive, and detach means use this value but do not pass slopes through it. After the temperature step, alpha is refreshed from log alpha, then each slow critic blends a small step toward its live critic.

7 How SAC is trained and how it compares

7.1 One gradient step for every step the arm takes

After every step the arm takes, one gradient step runs for the critics, the actor and alpha, then the slow copies blend.

Evaluation means testing the trained actor with exploration switched off. (Training DDPG: Target Networks, Replay and Noise) At evaluation the arm uses the centre of the bell, not a random draw.

The authors' code and Stable-Baselines3, a widely used reinforcement learning code library, both run this update. Both run the same SAC update, with two critics and a bell of torques.

The 2018a version tuned a reward scale per task instead; a reward scale means multiplying every reward by a number, which changes how much reward weighs against the entropy bonus. Automatic alpha replaces it.

7.3 SAC against other methods and on a real robot

The comparison includes Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), another way to train an actor: it makes small, careful updates and learns only from fresh steps. SAC reuses each stored step many times from its replay memory.

The comparison runs simulated robot tasks in MuJoCo, a physics simulator used for robot tasks, through Gym, a standard collection of reinforcement learning tasks. Each task is a simulated body learning to move: a one-legged hopper, a two-legged walker, a running half cheetah, a four-legged ant, a humanoid, and a harder humanoid from rllab, an older benchmark suite. Each body learns to move forward as fast as it can. It is compared with DDPG, PPO, soft Q-learning, an earlier method by the same group that also rewards staying undecided, and TD3.

The Minitaur senses its motor angles and how its body tips and turns. It is rewarded for walking forward and penalised for jerky or unsafe motion. It trained on flat ground only.

SAC is compared with a deterministic variant that does not reward staying undecided and closely resembles DDPG. A hard copy overwrites the slow copy with the live weights every so often. (Training DDPG: Target Networks, Replay and Noise) This variant keeps two critics, updates their slow copies by hard copies, has no slow actor and explores with fixed noise. SAC learned far more steadily than this variant from one run to the next.

A seed is one full training run started from its own random start.

Table 2. What the comparison found.
ExperimentResult
Simulated robot tasksSAC learned faster and reached higher returns on the hardest tasks, and DDPG made no progress on Ant, Humanoid and Humanoid (rllab). The automatic temperature tuning scheme "works well across all the environments, and thus effectively eliminates the need for tuning the temperature".
MinitaurLearned to walk from real-world steps alone, then walked up and down a slope, through wooden blocks and down stairs it had never trained on, with no further training.

The TD3 paper's comparison used an earlier SAC version; the TD3 paper notes the newer SAC, which also uses two critics and the lower score, is competitive with TD3 on most tasks. The version here adds automatic temperature tuning.

8 When SAC fits

SAC fits continuous actions where steps are costly, as on real robots, where exploration matters, or where settings must work across tasks without tuning. Prefer Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3) for a deterministic actor and slightly simpler code; Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) when fresh steps are cheap.
