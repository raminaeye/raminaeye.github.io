1 SimCLR learns from one big batch

SimCLR (Chen et al. 2020) trains a ResNet-50 on ImageNet and checks it with a linear probe, a small classifier trained on the frozen encoder, whose weights stay fixed. It edits each image twice, embeds every view through one shared encoder and head, and trains so each view picks its positive out of the batch. The loss is InfoNCE, taught on the loss page, not re-taught here. SimCLR runs every training step on one batch and carries only the weights between steps.

The probe on frozen features separates classes well, and fine-tuned on a small share of labels the pretrained model beats a supervised model trained on that share alone. Top-1 counts how often the model's single best guess is right; top-5 counts how often the right answer is among its five best guesses.

2 One batch becomes one loss

SimCLR turns a batch of \(N\) images into one loss. It edits each image twice, embeds every view, and trains the encoder so each view picks its positive out of all the others. One encoder \(f\) serves every view, followed by a small projection head \(g\). The projection head is a small network that turns each embedding into the projected embedding the loss reads.

The loss the walkthrough steps through is InfoNCE, taught in full on the loss page. In SimCLR terms: the score line says how alike two views are; the share line turns the scores into the positive's share of the batch; the loss line punishes a small share; and the batch line averages the loss over every view as anchor.

Figure 1. A toy batch; each step redraws the part of SimCLR it names.

Each view scores every candidate, the scores become probabilities, and training minimizes minus the log of the positive’s probability, averaged over every view.
\(\displaystyle \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlData{k=s}{\htmlStyle{color:#1f6fa8}{s_{i,k}}}} = \frac{\htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlData{k=sim}{\htmlStyle{color:#1f6fa8}{\operatorname{sim}(z_{i}, z_{k})}}}}{\htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlData{k=tau}{\htmlStyle{color:#8e44ad}{\tau}}}}\)
The score \(s_{i,k}\) compares anchor view \(i\) with candidate view \(k\). Each embedding \(z\) has length 1, so sim is their cosine similarity: at its highest when they point the same way, zero at right angles, and at its lowest when they point opposite. The temperature \(\tau\) divides every score. A small \(\tau\) stretches the gaps between scores, and the next line turns stretched gaps into lopsided shares, so the closest wrong views take most of the push. A large \(\tau\) flattens the gaps, and every negative is pushed about equally. CPC scored with a learned matrix instead and had no \(\tau\).
\(\displaystyle \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlData{k=p}{\htmlStyle{color:#1a6b3c}{p_{i,j}}}} = \frac{\htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlData{k=num}{\htmlStyle{color:#1a6b3c}{\exp(s_{i,j})}}}}{\htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlData{k=den}{\htmlStyle{color:#c0392b}{\sum_{k\in C} \exp(s_{i,k})}}}}\)
\(p_{i,j}\) is the probability the model gives to view \(i\)’s positive \(j\). exp raises \(e\) (about 2.718) to each score, so every term is positive and a higher score gets an exponentially larger one. \(C\) is the set of view \(i\)’s \(M\) candidates (\(\in\) means in), and \(\sum\) adds the terms of all of them. Dividing the positive’s term by that sum makes the shares add to 1, so raising the positive’s share must lower every negative’s; that coupling is what pushes negatives away. The fraction is a softmax share; InfoNCE is minus its log, on the next line. In SimCLR, \(C\) is every view except \(i\), so \(M = 2N - 1\); SimCLR calls the loss NT-Xent.
\(\displaystyle \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlData{k=ell}{\htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{\ell_{i,j}}}} = -\log \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlData{k=p}{\htmlStyle{color:#1a6b3c}{p_{i,j}}}}\)
The loss \(\ell _{i,j}\) of view \(i\) is minus the log of its positive’s share. It is near 0 when the positive wins clearly and grows without bound as \(p_{i,j}\) falls toward 0, so the views the model gets most wrong pull hardest. It is cross entropy with the positive as the right class.
\(\displaystyle \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlData{k=L}{\htmlStyle{color:#a84300}{L}}} = \frac{1}{2N} \sum_{i=1}^{2N} \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlData{k=ell}{\htmlStyle{color:#2c3e50}{\ell_{i,t(i)}}}}\)
Every view takes one turn as the anchor, so the loss \(L\) averages over all \(2N\) views. \(t(i)\) is the positive of view \(i\). Both views of a pair appear, so each pair is scored from both sides.

3 Augmentations decide what is learned

SimCLR edits every view with a random crop, color distortion and blur. Crops of one photo share the same mix of colors, so with crops alone the network matches color histograms (how often each color appears) and ignores shapes. Adding color distortion blocks that shortcut.

The snippet builds SimCLR’s edit pipeline in torchvision, and each call of augment draws fresh random edits.

def run(CROP_SIZE, FULL_STRENGTH, BLUR_SIZE, JIT_SHARE, HUE_SHARE, COLOR_P, GRAY_P, BLUR_P, PHOTO):  # pass your own sizes and settings
    import torch
    import torchvision.transforms as T

    SIZE, STRENGTH = CROP_SIZE, FULL_STRENGTH
    BLUR_K = BLUR_SIZE                 # odd, a small share of SIZE
    jit = JIT_SHARE * STRENGTH
    color = T.ColorJitter(jit, jit, jit, HUE_SHARE * STRENGTH)
    blur = T.GaussianBlur(BLUR_K)
    augment = T.Compose([
        T.RandomResizedCrop(SIZE),
        T.RandomHorizontalFlip(),
        T.RandomApply([color], p=COLOR_P),
        T.RandomGrayscale(p=GRAY_P),
        T.RandomApply([blur], p=BLUR_P),
        T.ToTensor(),
    ])

    img = T.ToPILImage()(torch.rand(3, PHOTO, PHOTO))   # toy photo
    view1, view2 = augment(img), augment(img)
  1. 1Edit settings for crop size, color strength and blur size; Figure 3’s slider scales STRENGTH, and raising it helps SimCLR and hurts a supervised model, because strong edits force the network to match content instead of pixels.
  2. 2The SimCLR edits in order (crop, flip, color, gray, blur), the edits of Figure 2.
  3. 3Two calls on one photo give a positive pair, view 1 and view 2 in Figure 3.

Weak edits teach little, because matching pixels is enough. Strong edits force the model to match content, until the crops share no scene and the two views no longer show the same thing. Edits that keep the label fight overfitting the same way they do in Data Augmentation for Wearables.

4 The projection head is thrown away after training

The encoder \(f\) outputs \(h\), the embedding you keep. A small projection head \(g\), an MLP (two linear layers with a ReLU, which zeroes negative values, between them), maps \(h\) to \(z\), and the loss reads only \(z\). SimCLR throws \(g\) away after training. The head learns to drop details the loss ignores, such as color and orientation, so \(h\) still holds them. The loss reads only \(z\), as taught on the InfoNCE page.

5 SimCLR in practice

This snippet runs one SimCLR training step, where both views pass through the encoder \(f\) and the head \(g\) of Section 4 and the loss reads only \(z\). \(D\) is the size of each embedding, the number of values in one \(z\).

def run(BATCH_SIZE, EMBED_DIM, TEMPERATURE, INPUT_DIM, HIDDEN, LR, MOMENTUM, WEIGHT_DECAY, IMG):  # pass your own sizes and settings
    import torch
    import torch.nn as nn
    import torch.nn.functional as F

    N, D, TAU = BATCH_SIZE, EMBED_DIM, TEMPERATURE
    f = nn.Sequential(nn.Flatten(), nn.Linear(INPUT_DIM, HIDDEN), nn.ReLU())
    g = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(HIDDEN, HIDDEN), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(HIDDEN, D))
    params = list(f.parameters()) + list(g.parameters())
    opt = torch.optim.SGD(params, lr=LR, momentum=MOMENTUM, weight_decay=WEIGHT_DECAY)

    x1, x2 = torch.rand(N, 3, IMG, IMG), torch.rand(N, 3, IMG, IMG)
    z = F.normalize(g(f(torch.cat([x1, x2]))), dim=1)   # [2N, D]
    s = z @ z.T / TAU
    s = s.masked_fill(torch.eye(2 * N, dtype=torch.bool), -torch.inf)
    t = torch.arange(2 * N).roll(N)
    loss = F.cross_entropy(s, t)
    opt.zero_grad()
    loss.backward()
    opt.step()
  1. 1Encoder \(f\), head \(g\) and one optimizer for both, the two boxes of Figure 1 step 2; SimCLR’s optimizer LARS is not in PyTorch, so SGD stands in.
  2. 2Both views through \(f\) then \(g\), giving unit-length \(z\), Figure 1 steps 1 and 2.
  3. 3NT-Xent on all 2\(N\) views, the InfoNCE page, Section 2 eq lines 1 to 4.
  4. 4One gradient step on \(f\) and \(g\), Figure 1 step 5.

One trap to avoid: BatchNorm, which normalizes each feature with its batch’s mean and variance, must use statistics from all devices (Global BN).

6 When to use SimCLR, and what MoCo fixes

Use SimCLR when a big batch fits in memory and the augmentation recipe is the lever to tune. When the batch cannot grow, the queue is the fix: MoCo keeps a queue of past keys instead of a giant batch, and it is the next page.
