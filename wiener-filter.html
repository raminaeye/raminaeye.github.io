The Wiener Filter

One gain per bin, set by how much of that bin is speech.

The Wiener filter cleans a noisy spectrogram by multiplying each bin by a gain between 0 and 1, once per STFT frame of the clip. The gain makes the cleaned value as close as possible to the clean speech on average (the smallest mean squared error), and it comes out as the share of the bin’s power that is speech. That share is unknown in practice and is guessed from the noisy signal, frame by frame.

1 The gain from two PSDs

As in Spectral Subtraction for Denoising, each STFT frame and bin holds a noisy value Y = S + D, speech plus noise, and a noise PSD PD (Power Spectral Density) is learned in the pauses. A hat marks an estimate.

The Wiener gain for one bin.
G = PSPS + PD
PS and PD are the speech and noise power in this bin, and G is the speech share of the power, between 0 and 1. A bin that is mostly speech keeps most of its value, and a bin that is mostly noise is turned down.
ξ = PSPD
ξ, the a priori SNR, is how much the speech outweighs the noise in this bin.
G = ξ1 + ξ
Divide top and bottom by PD. A large ξ gives G near 1 (keep the bin), a small ξ gives G near 0, and ξ = 1 (0 dB) gives G = 1/2.

G multiplies the amplitude, so the power left is G². Power subtraction keeps more at the same SNR.

Power subtraction’s share at the average noisy power.
1 − PD|Y|² ≈ ξ1 + ξ = G
Power subtraction keeps the power share 1 − PD/|Y|². It equals G exactly when |Y|² equals its average, (1 + ξ) PD, so its amplitude gain is √G, above Wiener’s G.

2 The gain across frequency

A gain per frequency is a filter’s magnitude response (How FIR and IIR Filters Shape Magnitude and Phase), with zero phase here, since the noisy angle is kept.

3 Why it is the best guess

A Bayesian estimate per bin treats the speech value S, before Y is seen, as a Gaussian (a bell curve) centered on 0 with power PS, the prior, and the noise as another Gaussian with power PD. Once Y is seen, Bayes’ rule (Naive Bayes) gives the posterior, the bell of values S could still have; the Gaussian conditioning example is on Probability and Bayes’ Rule.

The best guess in mean squared error.
Ŝ = E[ S | Y ]
E[ · ] is an average (an expectation) and the bar reads “given” (not a magnitude here): the best guess in mean squared error is the average of S over the posterior.
E[ S | Y ] = G · Y
With Gaussian speech and noise that average is Y scaled by the same Wiener gain G, so it shrinks toward 0 more when the noise is relatively bigger.

So under the Gaussian assumption no other per-bin rule has a smaller mean squared error; Wang and Chen (2018) call the Wiener filter the optimal estimator of speech in the power spectrum.

4 Wiener next to subtraction

PS is unknown, so ξ has to be guessed, and the simplest guess uses this frame alone. With it every rule becomes a curve in γ.

The one-frame SNR guess and the gain it gives.
γ = |Y|²PD
γ, the a posteriori SNR, is this frame’s bin power over the noise estimate.
ξ̂ = max(γ − 1, 0)
The noisy power minus the noise, over the noise, never below zero.
G = 1 − 1γ
Put into G = ξ/(1 + ξ), the guess gives this gain for γ ≥ 1. It is exactly the square of power subtraction’s gain, so the same noise bins survive, only dimmer, and the gain shape alone does not remove musical noise.

5 Guessing the SNR, and smoothing it

The one-frame guess jumps with every noise spike. Ephraim and Malah (1984) steadied it by mixing in last frame’s cleaned output, the decision-directed estimate, written here in the form Chinaev and Haeb-Umbach (2016) give.

The decision-directed SNR guess for frame m.
ξ̂m = a · |Ŝm−1|²PD
The first term trusts last frame's cleaned power over the noise; m − 1 is the previous frame and a is a weight near 1 (0.98 in the decision-directed paper). Speech changes slowly from frame to frame, so that cleaned estimate is a steadier guide than one noisy frame.
+ (1 − a) · max(γm − 1, 0)
Add a little of this frame’s own evidence, never below zero; with a = 0.98 a lone noise spike adds only 0.02 (γ − 1) for that frame. It is the one-pole smoother from How FIR and IIR Filters Shape Magnitude and Phase, run across frames.

The Wiener gain with the one-frame SNR guess, on the toy recording from Spectral Subtraction for Denoising.

import numpy as np
import librosa

SR, N_FFT, HOP = 8000, 256, 128
t = np.arange(2 * SR) / SR
voice = np.sin(2 * np.pi * 440 * t) * (t > 0.5)      # 440 Hz tone from 0.5 s
y = voice + 0.3 * np.random.default_rng(0).standard_normal(t.size)
Y = librosa.stft(y, n_fft=N_FFT, hop_length=HOP)     # (129, frames)
P_D = np.mean(np.abs(Y[:, 1:11]) ** 2, axis=1, keepdims=True)

gamma = np.abs(Y) ** 2 / P_D
xi = np.maximum(gamma - 1, 1e-3)                     # floor at -30 dB
G = xi / (1 + xi)
s_hat = librosa.istft(G * Y, hop_length=HOP, length=len(y))

The decision-directed guess as a function that runs one frame at a time, with a = 0.98.

import numpy as np

def dd_wiener(Y, P_D, A=0.98, XI_MIN=1e-3):
    S_hat = np.zeros_like(Y)
    prev = np.zeros(Y.shape[0])                      # last frame's cleaned power
    for m in range(Y.shape[1]):
        gamma = np.abs(Y[:, m]) ** 2 / P_D
        xi = A * prev / P_D + (1 - A) * np.maximum(gamma - 1, 0)
        xi = np.maximum(xi, XI_MIN)
        G = xi / (1 + xi)
        S_hat[:, m] = G * Y[:, m]
        prev = np.abs(S_hat[:, m]) ** 2
    return S_hat
  1. 1The smoothing weight a = 0.98 and the −30 dB floor.
  2. 2The output and last frame’s cleaned power, which starts at zero.
  3. 3One frame at a time, because each guess needs the frame before.
  4. 4γ for this frame.
  5. 5The decision-directed guess, both lines of the eq card in Section 5.
  6. 6The floor on ξ̂.
  7. 7The Wiener gain from the guess.
  8. 8Applies the gain and keeps this frame’s cleaned power for the next frame.

6.1 Gotchas

  • Floor the SNR guess above zero, or bins with γ ≤ 1 get a gain of exactly 0.

Modern denoisers train a network to predict a gain like this per bin, called a mask; the ideal ratio mask of supervised speech separation (References) is the speech share of the power, usually square-rooted, and without the root is exactly the Wiener gain.
