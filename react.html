ReAct: Thinking and Acting in One Loop

Thoughts stop the model guessing from memory and let facts steer the next move.

1 Thinking alone guesses

Ask a language model a question that needs a fact it never saw, and it answers anyway. The chain of thought helps it work through what it knows, but it cannot fetch what it does not know. More steps will not invent a missing fact, and a guess written early carries through to the answer.

ReAct gives the model a way out of its own memory. Between thoughts it acts: it searches, it looks a fact up, it reads what comes back. Each observation is a new fact from outside the model, and the next thought can build on it instead of on a guess.

It runs at inference, one step at a time until the answer. Nothing is trained. The full history of thoughts, actions and observations is the state the loop carries, and every new step reads the whole history back.

2 One loop for thoughts and actions

The loop has three parts, and they take turns. A thought plans or reads. An action reaches outside. An observation answers.

A thought is a sentence of reasoning the model writes to itself. It plans the next move, breaks the question apart, or reads an observation and says what follows from it. A thought changes nothing outside the model. It only joins the history, where later steps can use it.

An action is a call to the outside world. The question answering setup in the paper has three: Search, which returns the opening lines of a page; Lookup, which finds a sentence inside a page; and Finish, which ends the task with the answer.

An observation is what comes back after an action. It is text from the outside world, not from the model's memory: the lines a search returned, the sentence a lookup found. It is the only new fact in the loop. Everything else in the history, the model wrote itself.

The two directions feed each other. Thoughts say what to look up next, so the search lands on the right page. Observations say what the next thought can trust, so the plan stops guessing. That is the whole method: reason to act, and act to reason.

3 The loop in equations

Three lines carry the loop. The first says how the next step is picked. The second says what the choice is picked from. The third says what can be picked at all. The first line leans on the two after it. The thought step is the chain of thought from Chain of Thought: Thinking Before Answering, now used as one kind of action.

3.1 The policy picks the next step

A policy is the agent's rule for choosing a move, taught in RL Basics. Here the move is the next thought or the next action, and the situation it is chosen in is the whole history so far. The model itself plays the policy: prompted with the history, it writes the next step.

The policy over actions given context. How the next step is picked.
\(\displaystyle \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{\pi}} ( \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{\hat{a}_{t}}} \mid \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{c_{t}}} ) \)
Read it as a sentence: given the context so far, the policy gives a chance to each possible next step, and the next step is drawn from those chances. \(\pi\) is the policy, the prompted model. \(\hat{a}_{t}\) is the next step at step \(t\), a thought in words or an ordinary action. The bar reads given. \(c_{t}\) is the context, the full history, defined in the next line. Nothing here is trained: the frozen model plays the policy by continuing the prompt.
\(\pi\) the policy, the prompted model \(\hat{a}_{t}\) the next step, thought or action \(c_{t}\) the context, the history so far

3.2 The context is the whole history

The context is not a summary and not a state the model compresses. It is the trace itself, every step in order, and it grows by one entry each round.

The context as the full history. Everything the policy sees.
\(\displaystyle \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#5d6d7e}{c_{t}}} = ( \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#0e6b59}{o_{1}}}, \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{a_{1}}}, \dots, \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#0e6b59}{o_{t-1}}}, \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{a_{t-1}}}, \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#0e6b59}{o_{t}}} ) \)
Read it as a sentence: the context at step \(t\) is the whole history in order, ending with the latest observation. \(o\) is an observation, what the world sent back. \(a\) is an action the model made. The small \(t\) counts steps. The first entries are the earliest: the first observation, the first action, and so on down to \(o_{t}\), the observation that just arrived. Each round appends to this list, and the next call to the model reads the longer list. An action here covers a thought too, since the next line makes thoughts actions.
\(c_{t}\) the context at step \(t\) \(o\) an observation, from the world \(a\) an action, thought or call

3.3 Thoughts are actions too

This is the line that makes the loop possible. Ordinary actions reach the outside world. Thoughts do not, but the loop treats them as actions anyway, so the same policy that picks a search can pick a sentence of reasoning.

The augmented action space. Language joins the ordinary actions.
\(\displaystyle \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{\hat{\mathcal{A}}}} = \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#2471a3}{\mathcal{A}}} \cup \htmlClass{eqt}{\htmlStyle{color:#8e44ad}{\mathcal{L}}} \)
Read it as a sentence: the augmented action space is the ordinary actions together with all of language. \(\hat{\mathcal{A}}\), read A hat, is the augmented space, everything the policy can pick. \(\mathcal{A}\) is the ordinary action space: Search, Lookup, Finish. The cup sign reads union, everything in either side. \(\mathcal{L}\) is the space of language, every sentence the model could write. A thought is an action from \(\mathcal{L}\): it changes nothing outside, but it joins the context and steers the next pick. Language is open ended, so the method leans on a large model's feel for words rather than on training.
\(\hat{\mathcal{A}}\) the augmented space, all picks \(\mathcal{A}\) ordinary actions: Search, Lookup, Finish \(\mathcal{L}\) language, every sentence

One loop in code: the context grows by a thought, an action and an observation each round, and the search call is a real Wikipedia lookup.

import requests
def search(query):
    resp = requests.get("https://en.wikipedia.org/w/api.php", params={"action": "query", "list": "search", "srsearch": query, "format": "json"})
    return resp.json()["query"]["search"][0]["snippet"]
context = question
while True:
    ids = tok(context + "\nThought:", return_tensors="pt").input_ids
    step = tok.decode(model.generate(ids, max_new_tokens=LIMIT)[0], skip_special_tokens=True)
    action = step.split("Action:")[-1].strip().split("\n")[0]
    if action.startswith("Finish"):
        break
    context += "\n" + step + "\nObservation: " + search(action[action.index("[") + 1:action.index("]")])
  1. 1The search call, the paper's Wikipedia lookup in miniature.
  2. 2The starting context and the model call: context starts as the question alone, tok turns text into token ids and back, model is the prompted model, and LIMIT caps how much it may write in one step.
  3. 3The rest of the round: read the action out of the reply, stop on Finish, run the search and append the observation.

4 The Colorado orogeny walk

Here is the loop on a real question from the paper. The model gets the question only, with no passages attached. Every fact has to come through Search, Lookup or Finish, and the prompt shows a few full traces in this shape before the new question.

Question: What is the elevation range for the area that the eastern sector of the Colorado orogeny extends into?

Watch what the thoughts do. The first plans the whole route. The second reads the observation and spots what is missing. The third turns a found fact into the next search. The fourth fixes a search that came back vague. The fifth reads the range off the last observation and finishes. No thought guesses a fact; each one points at the next lookup.

A good search names the exact thing the thought needs. The vague search repeats the question's own words; the sharp one adds the words that pick out one region.

5 When thoughts stay sparse

The loop alternates thought and action every round, because each fact needs a fresh decision about what to fetch next. The trace above thinks at every step.

Long tasks cannot afford that. In a text game or a shop, the agent may take dozens of actions in a row: walking room to room, opening pages, picking options. Thoughts appear only where they help: breaking the goal into parts, tracking which parts are done, picking the next part, and using commonsense about where things are found. The model itself decides when to think and when to just act.

The trade is cost against control. A thought costs a model call and lengthens the history, so thinking everywhere would drown a long task. Thinking nowhere would leave the agent with no plan at all. Sparse thoughts keep the plan where it matters and let the actions run between them.

6 Practice

Each step costs one model call and one search. The context grows every round, so later calls read a longer prompt and cost more. There is no training run in the prompting setup: the model is frozen, and a few-shot prompt, one that shows a few full traces before the new question, does the teaching.

The headline setting is few-shot prompting with Thought, Action, Observation trajectories and a search API, from section 2 of the paper. The question answering runs use six hand-written traces as exemplars and a Wikipedia API with three actions: Search, Lookup and Finish.

The paper's own repository holds the prompts and the code behind the results, at github.com/ysymyth/ReAct. To try it, write a few traces in the Thought, Action, Observation shape, point the actions at a search API, and let the frozen model continue the trace.

7 Failure modes

Three failures cover most of what goes wrong. Each has a cause, a visible symptom, and a fix.

7.1 The loop repeats itself

Cause: the model writes the same thought and the same action again, instead of reasoning about what the last observation said. Symptom: the same search returns the same lines round after round, and the trace never moves on. Fix: stop the loop, rewrite the thought to say what the observation is missing, or fall back to a plain chain of thought for the answer.

7.2 The search returns nothing useful

Cause: the search names the wrong thing, or a name that covers more than one thing. Symptom: an observation with no fact the question needs, like the vague High Plains line in round 3. Fix: reformulate the search from the observation, as the trace does in round 4, adding the words that pick out the right page.

7.3 A thought strays from the facts

Cause: a thought is written from memory before any observation supports it, and the plan builds on the guess. Symptom: the trace reads smoothly while the facts underneath are wrong, and later searches chase the wrong thing. Fix: check each thought against the last observation before acting on it, and let an observation, not a hunch, set the next search.

8 What comes next

ReAct shows the loop works from a hand-written prompt and a few examples. But who teaches the model when to reach for a tool, instead of showing it in the prompt? Toolformer: A Model That Teaches Itself to Use Tools lets the model learn that choice from its own text, keeping only the tool calls that make the next words easier to predict.
