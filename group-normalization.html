Group Normalization and Instance Normalization

Split an image's channels into groups and normalize each group. One channel per group is InstanceNorm.

1 Big picture

Detection, segmentation and video models are big and their inputs are large, so only one or two images fit on each GPU, and there BatchNorm's error rises. Wu and He observed that a CNN's channels tend to come in related families, such as filters for related orientations and frequencies, so a group of channels within one image is a natural slice to normalize [1].

GroupNorm runs on every forward pass, per image, carries no state, and computes the same thing in training and at test time. Use it in CNNs with small per-GPU batches: detection, segmentation, video and diffusion U-Nets. Use InstanceNorm, its one-channel-per-group case, for style transfer and image-to-image generators.

2 GroupNorm

Split the \(C\) channels into \(G\) groups of \(C/G\) neighbors. Write \(g(c)\) for the group that channel \(c\) falls into.

Run the recipe on one image's channels that share a group.
\(\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\htmlClass{eqt sym-slice}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-slice)}{S_{n,g}}} = \{&(c', h', w') : {} \\ &g(c') = g\}\end{aligned}}\)
For image \(n\), every pixel of every channel in group \(g\); primes mean any value of that index, and \(i\) names one such cell.
\(\displaystyle {\htmlClass{eqt sym-mean}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-mean)}{\mu_{n,g}}} = \dfrac{1}{|\htmlClass{eqt sym-slice}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-slice)}{S_{n,g}}}|}\sum_{i \in \htmlClass{eqt sym-slice}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-slice)}{S_{n,g}}}} x_i}\)
The group's mean. \(|S|\) counts its cells: \(C/G\) channels times \(H\) times \(W\) pixels.
\(\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\htmlClass{eqt sym-var}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-var)}{\sigma^2_{n,g}}} &= \dfrac{1}{|\htmlClass{eqt sym-slice}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-slice)}{S_{n,g}}}|}\sum_{i \in \htmlClass{eqt sym-slice}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-slice)}{S_{n,g}}}}\big(x_i \\ &\qquad - \htmlClass{eqt sym-mean}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-mean)}{\mu_{n,g}}}\big)^2\end{aligned}}\)
Its spread around that mean.
\(\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\htmlClass{eqt sym-hat}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-hat)}{\hat{x}}} &= \dfrac{x - \htmlClass{eqt sym-mean}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-mean)}{\mu_{n,g(c)}}}}{\sqrt{\htmlClass{eqt sym-var}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-var)}{\sigma^2_{n,g(c)}}} + \htmlClass{eqt sym-eps}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-eps)}{\epsilon}}}} \\ y &= \htmlClass{eqt sym-gain}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-gain)}{\gamma_c}}\,\htmlClass{eqt sym-hat}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-hat)}{\hat{x}}} + \htmlClass{eqt sym-gain}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-gain)}{\beta_c}}\end{aligned}}\)
Normalize each value \(x\) (one cell \((n,c,h,w)\)) with its group's statistics, giving \(\hat{x}\), then apply a learned scale and shift per channel.
Only image \(n\) appears in the sums, so the batch size never enters: one image per GPU gives the same statistics as thirty-two.

3 From LayerNorm to InstanceNorm

The number of groups slides GroupNorm between two other layers. \(G = 1\) puts every channel in one group, which is LayerNorm over \(C\), \(H\) and \(W\). \(G = C\) gives each channel its own group, which is InstanceNorm. The paper's default is \(G = 32\) [1].

4 One image through GroupNorm

The line that runs at the current step is lit.
\(\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\htmlClass{eqt sym-slice}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-slice)}{S_{n,g}}} = \{&(c', h', w') : {} \\ &g(c') = g\}\end{aligned}}\)
The group: its channels, all their pixels, this image only.
\(\displaystyle {\htmlClass{eqt sym-mean}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-mean)}{\mu_{n,g}}},\ \htmlClass{eqt sym-var}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-var)}{\sigma^2_{n,g}}} \text{ over } \htmlClass{eqt sym-slice}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-slice)}{S_{n,g}}}}\)
The group's mean and spread.
\(\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\htmlClass{eqt sym-hat}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-hat)}{\hat{x}}} &= \dfrac{x - \htmlClass{eqt sym-mean}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-mean)}{\mu_{n,g(c)}}}}{\sqrt{\htmlClass{eqt sym-var}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-var)}{\sigma^2_{n,g(c)}}} + \htmlClass{eqt sym-eps}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-eps)}{\epsilon}}}} \\ y &= \htmlClass{eqt sym-gain}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-gain)}{\gamma_c}}\,\htmlClass{eqt sym-hat}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-hat)}{\hat{x}}} + \htmlClass{eqt sym-gain}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-gain)}{\beta_c}}\end{aligned}}\)
Normalize with the group's numbers, giving \(\hat{x}\); rescale per channel.

5 Slide the group count

One knob, the number of groups, moves GroupNorm from LayerNorm (one group) to InstanceNorm (one channel per group). Orange cells are above their group's mean, blue below.

6 Evidence

Wu and He trained ResNet-50 on ImageNet on 8 GPUs, varied the number of images on each GPU, and report top-1 error on the validation set [1].

At 2 images per GPU, GroupNorm's error is much lower than BatchNorm's, and at typical batch sizes the two are comparable. GroupNorm also beat its BatchNorm counterparts on COCO detection and segmentation and on Kinetics video classification [1].

7 InstanceNorm and style transfer

A fast style-transfer network turns a content photo into a painting in one forward pass. Its output should not depend on the photo's own contrast, so that the style can be painted on. Ulyanov et al. swapped BatchNorm for instance normalization and applied it at training and test time [2].

GroupNorm with one channel per group.
\(\displaystyle {\htmlClass{eqt sym-mean}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-mean)}{\mu_{n,c}}} = \dfrac{1}{HW}\sum_{h,w} x_{n,c,h,w}}\)
One channel of one image, averaged over its \(H\) times \(W\) pixels.
\(\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\htmlClass{eqt sym-var}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-var)}{\sigma^2_{n,c}}} &= \dfrac{1}{HW}\sum_{h,w}\big(x_{n,c,h,w} \\ &\qquad - \htmlClass{eqt sym-mean}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-mean)}{\mu_{n,c}}}\big)^2\end{aligned}}\)
That channel's spread.
\(\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\htmlClass{eqt sym-hat}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-hat)}{\hat{x}}} &= \dfrac{x - \htmlClass{eqt sym-mean}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-mean)}{\mu_{n,c}}}}{\sqrt{\htmlClass{eqt sym-var}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-var)}{\sigma^2_{n,c}}} + \htmlClass{eqt sym-eps}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-eps)}{\epsilon}}}} \\ y &= \htmlClass{eqt sym-gain}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-gain)}{\gamma_c}}\,\htmlClass{eqt sym-hat}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-hat)}{\hat{x}}} + \htmlClass{eqt sym-gain}{\htmlStyle{color:var(--sym-gain)}{\beta_c}}\end{aligned}}\)
Each value \(x\) of each channel of each image is centered and scaled on its own channel's statistics, giving \(\hat{x}\), so the image's contrast is removed.
The GroupNorm card with \(G = C\): the group of channel \(c\) is just \(c\).

nn.InstanceNorm2d defaults to affine=False and track_running_stats=False, so it learns no \(\gamma\) or \(\beta\) and uses each image's own statistics at test time too [4]. Adaptive instance normalization in StyleGAN-style generators keeps the normalization and replaces \(\gamma\) and \(\beta\) with values computed from a style vector.

9 In practice

nn.GroupNorm(num_groups, num_channels, eps=1e-5, affine=True): more groups move it toward InstanceNorm, fewer toward LayerNorm, and 32 groups is the paper's default [3]. torchvision's FCOS detection heads use GroupNorm with 32 groups as their default norm layer [5].

head = nn.Sequential(                         # one conv block of a detection head
    nn.Conv2d(256, 256, 3, padding=1),
    nn.GroupNorm(32, 256),                     # 32 groups of 8 channels
    nn.ReLU(),
)
x = torch.randn(2, 16, 8, 8)                   # (N, C, H, W): 2 images, 16 channels
C = x.shape[1]
gn1 = nn.GroupNorm(1, C, affine=False)
ln = nn.LayerNorm([C, 8, 8], elementwise_affine=False)
assert torch.allclose(gn1(x), ln(x), atol=1e-5)   # one group = LayerNorm
gnC = nn.GroupNorm(C, C, affine=False)
assert torch.allclose(gnC(x), nn.InstanceNorm2d(C)(x), atol=1e-5)  # one channel per group = InstanceNorm
class ResBlock(nn.Module):                     # style-transfer residual block
    def __init__(self, c):
        super().__init__()
        self.f = nn.Sequential(nn.Conv2d(c, c, 3, padding=1), nn.InstanceNorm2d(c, affine=True), nn.ReLU(), nn.Conv2d(c, c, 3, padding=1), nn.InstanceNorm2d(c, affine=True))
    def forward(self, x): return x + self.f(x)
  1. 1A detection-head conv block with GroupNorm: the same at 1 image per GPU as at 16.
  2. 2One group matches LayerNorm over (C, H, W) and one channel per group matches InstanceNorm, with the learned parts off. With them on, GroupNorm's \(\gamma\), \(\beta\) are per channel while LayerNorm's are per element, so the two match only at initialization.
  3. 3InstanceNorm inside a style-transfer network's residual block.
