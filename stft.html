The STFT and Spectrograms

A spectrum for every moment, and the trade-off between time and frequency detail.

The short-time Fourier transform (STFT) cuts a signal into short overlapping frames, fades the edges of each frame, and takes the FFT of each one. It runs once per frame, a new frame every 10 ms in a typical speech system, so one second is 100 FFTs. The output is a spectrogram, with time across, frequency up and brightness for strength, and the frame length trades sharp timing against sharp pitch.

1 One spectrum for the whole clip hides when things happen

A rising and a falling siren hold the same frequencies in opposite order, and one spectrum of the whole clip adds every moment together.

2 Frames, windows and the DFT

Cut the signal into short frames of L samples, each starting R samples (the hop) after the last. Fade each frame’s edges with a window w[n], take its DFT, and stand the result up as one column.

Frames usually overlap by a half or three quarters. The DFT size N can exceed L by padding with zeros, which adds bins but no detail.

3 Time resolution and frequency resolution

Dennis Gabor showed in 1946 that no signal is sharp in both time and frequency at once. In an STFT the window length L sets the balance.

What one window length buys and what it costs.
Δf = fsL
Bins in one column sit Δf hertz apart. At fs = 8 kHz, L = 64 gives 125 Hz and L = 1,024 gives 7.8 Hz, so two pitches closer than about Δf share one band. Zero-padding to a DFT size N above L adds bins but not this resolution, because the frame still holds L real samples.
Tw = Lfs
The window spans Tw seconds, and everything inside it lands in the same column, so a click spreads over that span: 8 ms for 64 samples, 128 ms for 1,024. Multiply the two lines and Δf times Tw is 1: raising L sharpens frequency and blurs time by the same factor.
Δt = Rfs
Columns sit Δt seconds apart, one hop. A smaller hop R draws more columns per second but cannot undo the spread of line 2, because each column still sums a whole window.

4 Windows and leakage

The DFT treats a frame as one period of a signal that repeats forever.

A jump contains every frequency, so a tone that does not fit a whole number of cycles spills energy into far bins. This is leakage. A window fades the frame toward zero at both ends so the copies meet smoothly; the plain cut is the rectangular window, w[n] = 1.

Three windows of length L, each a short sum of cosines.
w[n] = 12 (1 − cos 2πnL)
Hann: one raised cosine, zero at the frame’s start, one in the middle and back toward zero at the end, so the copies meet without a jump. Its highest side lobe is 31.5 dB down and the lobes keep falling 18 dB per octave, but the main lobe is 4 bins wide, twice the plain cut’s 2.
w[n] = 0.54 − 0.46 cos 2πnL
Hamming: the same cosine on a step, so the ends sit at 0.08 instead of 0. The weights 0.54 and 0.46 are picked so the first side lobe nearly cancels, 42.5 dB down here, but because the ends do not reach zero the far lobes fall only 6 dB per octave.
w[n] = 0.42 − 0.5 cos 2πnL
Blackman, first part: a Hann-like cosine with a smaller constant.
  + 0.08 cos 4πnL
A second cosine at twice the speed flattens the ends further. The highest side lobe drops to 58 dB down and the main lobe widens to 6 bins, so close tones blur more than with Hann.

5 Going back with overlap-add

Denoising edits a spectrogram and then needs the sound back: take the inverse DFT of every column, slide each frame back into place and add the overlaps.

The condition for overlap-add to give the signal back.
Σm w[n − mR] = constant
Overlap-add gives the signal back only when the shifted windows add up to a flat line, so every sample gets the same total weight. Hann at 50% overlap does, because where one window fades out the next fades in by the same amount.

Jont Allen and Lawrence Rabiner tied overlap-add and filter-bank resynthesis together in 1977.

The round trip in SciPy, with the largest error printed at the end.

import numpy as np
from scipy import signal

FS, L, HOP = 16000, 400, 200
print(signal.check_COLA('hann', L, L - HOP))
x = np.random.default_rng(0).standard_normal(FS)
_, _, Z = signal.stft(x, fs=FS, window='hann', nperseg=L, noverlap=L - HOP)
_, x_back = signal.istft(Z, fs=FS, window='hann', nperseg=L, noverlap=L - HOP)
print(np.max(np.abs(x - x_back[:len(x)])))         # 1.8e-15, near machine precision
  1. 1Sets a 400-sample Hann window with hop 200 and checks that the windows sum flat (eq card in Section 5, line 1).
  2. 2Makes one second of noise and takes its STFT (eq card in Section 2, lines 1 and 2).
  3. 3Overlap-adds the frames back (Figure 8) and prints the largest error, 1.8e-15, near machine precision.

This is how Spectral Subtraction for Denoising rebuilds audio after cleaning the spectrogram.

6 In practice

Speech systems usually take 25 ms frames every 10 ms. At 16 kHz that is 400 samples with a hop of 160, and the DFT size is the next power of two, 512. Conformer starts from features on exactly such 10 ms frames, and CTC Loss lines labels up with them.

The speech settings in librosa, window L = 400 and hop R = 160. One second of samples goes in, and the complex STFT comes out with 257 bins by 101 frames.

import numpy as np
import librosa

SR, N_FFT, L, R = 16000, 512, 400, 160
x = np.random.default_rng(0).standard_normal(SR)
X = librosa.stft(x, n_fft=N_FFT, hop_length=R, win_length=L)
power = np.abs(X) ** 2
print(X.shape)                      # (257, 101)
  1. 1Sets the speech settings, 25 ms windows every 10 ms at 16 kHz, and one second of noise.
  2. 2Takes the STFT with L = 400 and R = 160 (eq card in Section 2, lines 1 and 2).
  3. 3Keeps the power of each bin and prints 257 bins by 101 frames.

6.1 Gotchas

  • librosa pads the signal so each frame is centered on its hop position; set center=False to start frames there instead.
  • Inverting needs the same window, hop and length as the forward call.
